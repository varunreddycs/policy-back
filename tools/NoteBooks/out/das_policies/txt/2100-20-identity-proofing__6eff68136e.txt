I. P urpose  
The purpose of this policy is to provide guidance for the use of Identity Proofing  for Non-
Organizational User s associated with the state’s Identity Management System . The first 
occurrence of the word in the policy body outside of a title or heading is in bold, italic type, and is 
hyperlinked to the definition in Section IV.   
II. Sc ope  
The scope of this policy includes all Non-Organizational User  accounts in the state’s Identity 
Management System . This policy represents the minimum acceptable criteria governing the 
automatic creation of all Non-Organizational User accounts.  
III. P olicy  
The Ohio Department of Administrative Services (DAS) Office of Information Technology (OIT) shall 
provide Identity Proofing services, which meet the requirements outlined in this policy. 
A. I dentity Proofing  Non-Organizational User Accounts: Each application owner shall 
determine Identity Proofing  requirements based on the risk of harm associated with the 
transactions processed in their respective application and based on the results of their data 
classification. Potential harms include identity theft, fraud, unauthorized disclosure of 
information, and account takeover. 
 
1. Low Risk of Harm Transactions: Identity Proofing  of Non -Organizational User s is not 
required when there is little or no risk of harm to the state, users, or others. For example, 
giving the state funds or information is typically low risk.

2. Moderate or High Risk of Harm Transactions: Identity  Proofing of Non -Organizational 
Users is required when there is a moderate or high risk of harm to the state, users, or 
others. For example, transactions containing financial or Confidential Data.  
3. Migration of Existing Users:  Non-Organizational Users of an application may be migrated 
into the system and may not require additional Identity Proofing  to access that 
application. However, these users may require Identity Proofing  to access additional 
applications, as determined by each application owner. 
4. Authorized Business Representatives: Businesses registered to use the identity system 
shall be required to complete business Identity Proofing.   
a) During this process, an Authorized Business Representative is identified and shall be 
required to complete Identity Proofing. This person will have the ability to assign 
and approve elevated privileges, and to create additional Privileged User s and 
general users within their business accounts.  
b) Any additional users associated with a business account shall also be required to 
complete Identity Proofing before becoming Privileged Users. 
 
B. Identity Re-Proofing: Citizens, businesses, and Authorized Business Representatives shall be 
required to undergo identity re-proofing under the following circumstances: 
 
1. Citizen Re-Proofing: Citizens shall be required to repeat Identity Proofing when their core 
identity information changes, as it relates to the information used to provide assurance 
of a citizen’s identity, such as: 
a) First Name 
b) Last Name 
c) Date of Birth 
d) Social Security Number or similar National Identification Number 
2. Business Re -Proofing: Businesses shall be required to repeat Identity Proofing  when 
there is a change to their core business identifiers, as it relates to the information used to 
provide assurance of a business’ identity. For example, changes to the following: 
a) Federal Employer Identification Number (FEIN) 
b) Business name  
c) Business address 
3. Authorized Business Representatives Re-Proofing: Authorized Business Representatives 
shall be required to repeat Identity Proofing when there is a change to their core identity 
information, as it relates to information used to provide assurance of a business 
representative’s identity, such as:  
a) First Name 
b) Last Name 
c) Date of Birth

4. Identity Re -Proofing for Identity Theft: Identity re -proofing shall be required when a 
citizen or business representative’s identity is flagged as a victim of identity theft. 
5. Business/Application Owner Identity Re -Proofing Requirements: Business/application 
owners are authorized to require Identity Proofing frequencies or conditions for Identity 
Proofing that are more stringent than the requirements of this policy. These decisions 
shall be based upon the information contained in the application or the sensitivity of the 
business transactions that are conducted through the use of the application. 
 
C. Identity Validations: When high-risk financial transactions are involved , a business owner 
requests validation based on their data classification results, or if a user wishes to opt in to 
additional security measures. The exception process shall be followed whenever Identity 
Validations are used in place of Identity Proofing.  
 
Below are examples of Identity Validation s which can be used to ensure a registered user is 
the person authorized to complete a specific transaction. 
 
1. Re-authentication: A re -authentication process shall be available, which can be 
employed to complete sensitive transactions. 
2. Two-Step Authentication: A Two-Step Authentication process shall be available, which 
can be employed to complete sensitive transactions. Examples of Two -Step 
Authentication include: 
a) Sending an authentication code to a mobile phone number on file. 
b) Requiring the end-user to answer security questions on file. 
 
D. Notification Messages: Notification messages, such as postal letters, emails, or notifications 
at login, shall be available to inform a user when changes are made to their state profile. This 
would include offering a means of redress in the event that their state identity was 
compromised and used without their knowledge. 
 
E. Identity Proofing Process: Identity Proofing shall be completed using an automated process, 
a manual process, or a combination of automated and manual processes. 
 
Alternative manual Identity Proofing  processes shall be established that meet a burden of 
proof similar to that of an automated Identity Proofing process. In the event of a failure of an 
automated Identity Proofing process (e.g., an issue with the information associated with the 
individual completing the process), the application owner has discretion to approve 
application access without completing an Identity Proofing  process. This approval does not

IV. Definitions 
  
A. Authorized Business Representative. For the purposes of this policy, an individual who is 
appointed by a business to initiate, modify or complete transactions with the state on behalf 
of the business. This designation provides the person(s) with the ability to assign and approve 
elevated privileges, and to create additional Privileged Users and general users within their 
business accounts that also have the ability to initiate, modify or complete transactions with 
the State of Ohio. 
B. Confidential Data. This classification includes Data that is required to be protected by law or 
regulation, is intended for confidential use, and may not be copied or removed from an 
agency's operational control without authorized permission.  This classification includes Data 
that, if compromised, may result in loss of life, serious injury, or other harm to an individual 
or group, or disruption to critical agency operations. 
C. Identity Management System. An Identity Management System is comprised of one or more 
systems or applications that manage the identity verification, validation, and issuance 
process.

D. Identity Proofing . The process of providing sufficient information (e.g., identity history, 
credentials, documents) to establish an identity.2 
E. Identity Validation. Tests enabling an information system to authenticate users or resources.3 
F. Non-Organizational User. A user who is not an Organizational User, which for the purposes of 
this policy includes public users.

otherwise authorized to work on behalf of the State, and who has a need to access State of
