I. Purpose 
This policy provides requirements for how Agencies  standardize, obtain, and safeguard certain  
Domain Names by establishing naming requirements, and a central registration, tracking, and 
management process. The first occurrence of a defined term in the policy body outside of a title or 
heading is in bold, italic type, and is hyperlinked to the definition in Section IV. 
 
II. Scope 
This policy applies to all state Agencies , boards, and commissions under the authority of the  
Governor (collectively referred to as Agency or Agencies). 
 
III. Policy 
In the interest of establishing strong brand recognition, minimizing confusion for U sers, 
simplifying statewide Domain Name management, and improving overall security, Agencies shall 
follow the registration, naming, and management requirements outlined in this policy. 
 
A. .gov Requirements:  Agencies shall register all State  of Ohio-related Internet sites under a 
.gov domain.  
 
1. When requesting a .gov Domain Name, the word “Ohio” shall be  included within the 
Second-Level Domain (SLD). 
2. Agencies are encouraged to request  Third-Level Domain  “Ohio.gov” Domain Names 
(e.g., OAKS.Ohio.gov) rather than pursuing unique .gov SLDs. Registering a Third-L evel 
“Ohio.gov” domain avoids a registration fee , ensures the domain will never expir e, and 
clearly associates the domain with the “Ohio.gov” brand.

B. .gov  Naming Requirements: Agencies shall ensure that they adhere to the following .gov 
naming requirements, which are in alignment with  the DOTGOV Online Trust in Government 
Act of 2020 (6 U.S.C. § 665) and the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) Cybersecurity and 
Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) DotGov Program Requirements: 
 
1. T
he .gov domain shall not be used to advertise for private individuals, firms, or  
corporations, or imply in any manner that the government endorses or favors any specific 
commercial product, commodity, or service.  
2. The .gov domain shall not be used for campaigning. The .gov websites may not be directly 
linked to or refer to websites created or operated by a campaign or any campaign entity 
or committee. No political sites, party names, acronyms or political causes shall be used.  
3. Agencies shall avoid the use of “slang” within a Domain Name. For example, if a website 
is referring to a regulatory process, requesting “ohiofileupdates.gov” as a domain would 
be considered “slang,” or using terms such as "commish” instead of “commission.” 
4. Agencies shall avoid the use of underscores within a Domain Name, and spaces are 
prohibited. Although not recommended, hyphens may be used, except at the beginning 
or end of a Domain Name. 
5. Agencies shall avoid the use of n ames, acronyms, or abbreviations  that are generic in 
nature or associated with, easily confused with, or too similar to those of another Agency, 
organization, or entity, or that could be interpreted as disparaging to a group or 
classification of Ohio residents. 
6. A .gov domain must not be used to distribute or promote material when the distribution 
violates applicable law. 
7. The .gov domain must not be used to distribute malware, host open redirects, or  
otherwise engage in malicious cyber activity. 
8. The DHS CISA DotGov program and/or the Ohio Department of Administrative Services 
(DAS) Office of Information Technology (OIT) reserves the right to terminate any .gov 
domain that is not in compliance with the requirements set forth in this policy as well as 
the .gov domain requirements published by the DHS CISA. 
 
C. U
se of Alternate Top -Level Domains (TLD): If an Agency elects to use a Top- Level  Domain 
(TLD) o ther than .gov, the Agency shall: 
 
1. Sub
mit all non-.gov domain registration requests to DAS OIT Unified Network Services 
(UNS) through the DAS OIT Ordering System.  
2. Ensure that Users are redirected to an Ohio.gov domain.

D. Verify Domain Name Availability : A gencies shall ensure that any desired Domain Name is 
available for acquisition. If it is determined that it is not feasible to obtain the desired Domain 
Name, the requesting Agency  shall select another . The requesting Agency  shall ensure the 
availability of a Domain Name before any marketing investments are made and/or any official 
notifications are distributed. 
 
E. P urchase and Administration of Domain Names : DAS shall purchase and administer 
domains on behalf of Agencies as well as any other state entities interested in using the DAS 
OIT service.  
 
F. Do
main Name Maintenance: DAS OIT UNS shall maintain the internal and external Domain 
Name Service for the State of Ohio.   
 
1. On
ly DAS OIT managed Domain Name servers shall be used to host and support all 
registered domains. 
2. The DAS OIT Office of Information Security and Privacy (OISP) shall review all new  
Domain Name requests and determine if a new Domain-Based Message Authentication, 
Reporting and Conformance (DMARC) record needs to be created  to help ensure email 
security. 
a) If a new DMARC record is required, DAS  OIT OISP shall initiate and oversee its  
creation and implementation. 
b) DAS OIT OISP shall annually audit DNS records to confirm a corresponding DMARC 
record exists. 
3. Domain Names shall be administered and renewed until a request for cancellation is 
received via the online DAS OIT Ordering System.  
a) DAS OIT shall retain a Domain Name registration for a minimum of 12 months after 
a Web service is discontinued or migrated to another Domain Name. 
b) During this 12 -month period, a notice with the new Domain Name or a notice of 
service discontinuation shall be posted on  the website of the Domain Name to be 
cancelled. 
4. DAS OIT UNS may delegate the administration of the Domain Name System (DNS) Entries 
to an Agency but must maintain oversight for those entries.  
a) The DAS OIT UNS team shall audit any delegated DNS Entries at least once every 12 
months to validate authenticity and consistenc y and to help mitigate the risk of 
domain hijacking.  
b) The Agency that is granted delegated administration shall regularly perform audits 
and maintain documentation concerning  the DNS Zone(s). This documentation 
shall include, but not be limited to, a description of the service, current Internet

d) Any violation of account protection or secure connection requirements shall result

G. Policy Exceptions:  Any requests for exceptions to the Domain Name requirements outlined 
in this policy shall be submitted to DAS OIT  for review and consideration (refer to section VII. 
Inquiries for the State IT Policy Manager contact information). 
 
H. P rocedures : Agencies interested in registering or cancelling a Domain Name shall follow the 
procedures outlined below. 
 
1. Age
ncies shall establish a primary and secondary administrative contact for managing 
Domain Names. 
a) DAS OIT UNS shall be notified of changes in primary and/or secondary contacts  as 
soon as practicable. In addition, DAS OIT UNS shall validate the list of Agency 
contacts on an annual basis. 
b) The administrative contact shall request desired Domain Names by accessing DAS 
OIT’s Ordering System  and selecting “Telecommunication Services/Domain 
Services.” A justification for the domain and IP Address shall be included in the DAS 
OIT’s Ordering System request. 
c) The administrative contact shall be responsible for providing all required 
information on the Domain Services order form. 
d) In addition, the Agency administrative contact shall provide any relevant procedure 
or domain inventory updates to DAS OIT UNS as soon as possible.   
2. The requested D omain Name(s) shall be reviewed by DAS OIT UNS  and, if approved, 
acquired by DAS through an approved registrar for the requested TLD. 
3. Upon successfully registering a requested Domain Name, notification will be sent to the 
Agency administrative contact. 
4. To cancel a Domain Name, the Agency administrative contact shall access the DAS OIT’s 
Ordering System and select “remove domain” from the menu on the Domain Services 
order form. 
5. DAS OIT UNS  shall invoice Agencies  via a voucher of intrastate payment for all costs 
associated with registration and administration of Domain Names. 
 
IV. Definitions 
 
A. DAS-Managed System Assets . Information, hardware, software , and services required to 
support state business, and identified during the risk assessment process as assets that need 
to be protected. Primary responsibility for managing these system assets may be assigned to 
DAS OIT personnel or outside entities. 
B. Domain Name. A Domain Name is used to locate computers on the Internet and to allow users 
to reach web sites. Each Domain Name has at least two components, the T op-Level Domain

Name and the secondary level Domain Name. Structurally, TLDs and SLDs are combined to 
create a Domain Name address: www.secondlevel.toplevel. 
C. Domain-Based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance (DMARC) . An email 
authentication method that i mproves security by helping  mitigate common cyber threats, 
including email Spoofing and Phishing. 
D. DAS OIT’s Ordering System . DAS OIT’s Ordering System allows users to order statewide  
services online, including telecommunication services such as domain services, Wireless as a 
Service, mobile services, and Internet access. These requests are submitted online in 
ServiceNow. 
E. Domain Name Service. The Ohio Department of Administrative Services Office of Information 
Technology Unified Network Services provides a domain name service to Agencies as well as 
any other interested state entities. The service offering includes installing, administering, and 
maintaining internal and external Domain Name System servers for customers.   
F. Domain Name System (DNS). The system by which Internet domain names and addresses are 
tracked and regulated.1 
G. Domain Name System (DNS) Entries. A DNS Entry is any number of records that can be added 
to a DNS Z one or subzone/child zone. These entries can be in the form of a mapping of the 
zone to the corresponding Internet Protocol Addresses (A R ecord), a mapping to a name 
server (NS Record), or a text entry for domain validation (TXT Record). 
H. Domain Name System (DNS) Zone. A logical organizational structure for the DNS that allows 
for the management of granular components by an administrator.  
I. Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol (DHCP). The automatic assignment of Internet Protocol 
addresses to network connected devices. 
J. Internet. A worldwide system of computer networks - a network of networks - in which 
computer users can get information and access services from other computers. The Internet 
is generally considered to be public, untrusted, and outside the boundary of the State of Ohio 
network. 
K. Internet Protocol (IP) Address. A unique number that is a four (IPv4) or eight (IPv6) octet source 
or destination number, which identifies a computing device on a network.  
L. Internet Protocol (IP) Address Management  (IPAM). The practice of planning and managing 
the IP address space on a network.
