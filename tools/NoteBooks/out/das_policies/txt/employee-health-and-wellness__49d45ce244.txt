Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to set forth procedures and guidance for management and employees that address employee health and wellness. The first occurrence of a defined term in the policy is in bold, italic type.

Scope

This policy applies to all state agencies, boards, and commissions under the authority of the Governor (collectively referred to as Agency or Agencies).

Policy

The State of Ohio is committed to promoting the health and wellness of all its employees. The Joint Healthcare Committee (JHCC), made up of equal members of management and labor, also works in support of the efforts to promote the health and wellness of state employees. Employees must comply with any Agency or program instructions when participating in wellness activities, including providing accurate information when required.

In partnership with the Ohio Department of Administrative Services (DAS), Agencies have a role in administering the employee health and wellness program.

Take Charge | Live Well

The State of Ohio's employee health and wellness program is Take Charge | Live Well (or, TCLW). TCLW is designed to support the practice of healthy habits that contribute to an individual’s attainment of physical, mental, and social wellbeing. The program promotes employee participation in health assessments, health screenings, and other activities that contribute to an individual’s overall healthy lifestyle.

The DAS Benefits Administration Services team (BAS) administers TCLW, and the team can assist Agencies in developing Agency-specific health and wellness plans. The Take Charge | Live Well web site is available at
Take Charge - Live Well - Department of Administrative Services
.

Agency Health and Wellness Program Responsibilities

The following list details Agency responsibilities for the employee health and wellness program under this policy.

Agencies shall provide strong and visible leadership from the senior management level to Take Charge | Live Well and the Agency’s health and wellness initiatives by encouraging employee participation in activities and reducing barriers to participation.

Agencies shall appoint a Wellness Ambassador. The Wellness Ambassador’s responsibilities include but are not limited to:

Assist management with including employee health and wellness as part of the Agency’s goals.

Serve as liaison to BAS and TCLW.

Serve as a communication and coordination liaison to their Agency and all its locations for TCLW initiatives.

Schedule and promote health and wellness-related events.

Attend quarterly statewide wellness coordinator meetings, webinars, and/or conference calls.

Agencies with 50 or less employees are not required to designate a Wellness Ambassador. DAS will assume wellness programming and event coordination for such Agencies.

Agencies may establish a health and wellness committee or multiple health and wellness committees depending on the size and number of locations. Guidance on such committees can be found in Section 20.03 (F) of the collective bargaining agreement between the State of Ohio and the Ohio Civil Service Employees Association (OCSEA).

Use of Time and State Resources for Employee Wellness

If operational needs permit, Agencies are encouraged to allow employees to participate in Take Charge | Live Well activities while on State time and using State resources. When an Agency permits employees to participate in such activities on State time or using State resources, the Agency may determine a reasonable amount of time permitted for an activity. Employees must coordinate with their supervisors and comply with all Agency policies while participating in wellness activities. Where permitted by the Agency, employees may use reasonable state time and resources for the following types of health and wellness activities (this is not an exhaustive list):

Computers and Web Access – Agencies, to the extent possible, may make employees’ work computers available to them in support of employee wellness initiatives, such as completion of an online personal health assessment, lifestyle change programs, webinars, and surveys through Take Charge | Live Well.

Health Screenings – Biometric health screenings with follow-up counseling are available at many State work sites annually.

Health Fairs and Educational Seminars – Agencies may offer health fairs to educate employees on health, prevention, and wellness resources.

Prevention Programs – Programs or course work for certain health conditions that may continue over an extended period of time (e.g., Diabetes Prevention Program).

Onsite vaccination clinics and blood drives.

Other Health and Wellness Resources

Health and Wellness Activities – Subject to Agency operational needs and Agency policy, employees are encouraged to use break times and meal periods to participate in wellness activities and Agencies may designate a location for them in State owned and leased spaces if available. Such activities include, but are not limited to:

Visiting a gym.

Walking, jogging, stretching exercises, or other similar physical activity.

Attending yoga, meditation, weight loss, exercise, or other health-related and/or overall wellness related activities (e.g., wellness videos or financial education resources offered by the wellness program provider).

Nutrition – Agencies are encouraged to make healthy foods and beverages available in vending machines, cafeterias, and snack bars. Clear identification and prominent display of healthy choices are encouraged.

Liability

Agencies should be aware of and address liability issues associated with health and wellness activities. Depending on the activity, this may require coordination and discussion with the appropriate Legal, Finance, and/or Human Resources staff to ensure efforts are made to address risk and liability issues as well as to ensure compliance with Agency policies.

Use of participant waivers.

Use of State facilities.

Use of State time or restriction on use of State time.

Vendor contracts, including Agency procurement requirements.

Definitions

None.
