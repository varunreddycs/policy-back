Katrina Flory, Assistant Director/State CIO 
I. Purpose 
This policy defines the requirements for developing an effective Security Plan , including the 
selection of security controls and enhancements for Ohio Department of Administrative Services 
(DAS)-Managed information systems.  
 
The first occurrence of the word in the policy body outside of a title or heading is in bold, italic type, 
and is hyperlinked to the definition in Section IV . In addition, references to National Institute of 
Standards and Technology (NIST) Special Publication (SP) 800 -53, “Security and Privacy Controls 
for Federal Information Systems and Organizations,” family identifiers and control numbers are 
provided in parentheticals next to requirement headers, where applicable. The requirements of 
the NIST SP 800-53 Planning (PL) control family are addressed in this policy. 
 
II. Scope 
The scope of this policy includes all DAS -managed information systems and the DAS S ystem and 
Service Owners who maintain them.  
 
III. Policy 
DAS is issuing this policy to protect DAS -managed information systems. DAS system and Service 
Owners shall ensure that security planning documents are developed and maintained for all DAS-
managed information systems. 
 
A. System Security Plan (PL- 2): All system Security Plans shall align with the requirements 
outlined in DAS Standard DAS-ITS-2100-23-A, “System Security Plan Standard.

B. Rules of Behavior (PL-4): Ohio Administrative Policy IT-04, “Use of Internet, E-mail and Other 
IT Resources,” as well as DAS Policy 700 -01, “Information Technology Resource Usage,” 
outline the rules of behavior for individuals who access the state’s information systems. These 
rules describe user responsibilities and expected behavior with regard to information and 
information system usage.  
 
C. Information Security Architecture (PL-8) : An information security architecture shall be 
developed, describing the overall philosophy, requirements and approach to protecting the 
Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability of system information. The information security 
architecture shall be integrated into and support the enterprise architecture. Information 
security assumptions about, and dependencies on, external services shall be included in the 
information security architecture. 
 
1. The information security architecture shall be updated when significant changes occur, 
or at least annually, to reflect updates in the enterprise architecture. 
2. Information security architecture changes shall be reflected in the Security Plan and any 
applicable procurement/acquisitions policies or contractual terms and conditions. 
3. The information security architecture shall reflect a defense-in- depth approach, which 
requires layers of security controls. 
a) The security controls shall operate in a coordinated and mutually reinforcing 
manner. 
b) Layered security controls shall be obtained from different suppliers, to further 
expand the defense-in-depth approach. 
 
D. Compliance: Security planning requirements for DAS-managed information systems shall be 
reviewed any time there is a contract renewal, significant changes to a DAS-Managed System 
Asset or following a significant security incident.  
 
A significant change is defined as a substantial modification that alters the mission, 
configuration or basic vulnerabilities of the DAS -Managed System Asset. Changes that may 
indicate the need for a new risk assessment and an updated Security Plan  include new or 
significantly changed:  
 
1. Business Requirements  
2. Hardware 
3. Application Programs 
4. External Users  
5. Telecommunications

IV. Definitions 
  
A. Availability. Ensuring timely and reliable access to and use of information.1   
B. Confidentiality. Preserving authorized restrictions on information access and disclosure, 
including means for protecting personal privacy and proprietary information. 2  
C. DAS-Managed System Asset . Information, hardware, software and services required to 
support state business, and identified during the risk assessment process as assets that need 
to be protected. Primary responsibility for managing these system assets may be assigned to 
DAS OIT personnel or other outside entities. 
D. Integrity. Guarding against improper information modification or destruction, and includes 
ensuring information non-repudiation and authenticity.
3 
E. Security Plan. Formal document that provides an overview of the security requirements for an 
information system or an information security program and describes the security controls in 
place or planned for meeting those requirements.
4 
F. Service Owner . A Service Owner is responsible for the delivery (design, performance, 
integration), continual improvement and management of assigned IT services. 
G. System Owner . Person responsible for the development, procurement, integration, 
modification, operation, and maintenance, and/or final disposition of an information system.5
