                  Human Trafficking Prevention and Awareness (HR-41)


May 03, 2024

 |

Agency

State of Ohio Administrative Policy

No: HR-41 -
State Human Resources Division

Effective:
May 3, 2024

Issued By:

Kathleen C. Madden, Director

Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to increase awareness among State of Ohio employees of the
Human Trafficking
 problem and set forth requirements for training regarding Human Trafficking as recommended by the Ohio Human Trafficking Task Force (Task Force). The Task Force was created by an Executive Order of the Governor in 2012 and the Task Force made its original training recommendations later that year. The Task Force’s goals and activities were continued through the issuance of Executive Order 2021-02D. The first occurrence of a defined term in the policy is in bold, italic type, and is hyperlinked to the definition in Section IV.

Scope

This policy applies to all state agencies, boards, and commissions under the authority of the Governor (collectively referred to as Agency or Agencies).

Policy

State employees in many positions and Agencies across Ohio may come into contact with victims of Human Trafficking. It is the policy of the State of Ohio that state employees are equipped with the knowledge and resources necessary to assist in addressing the problem of Human Trafficking in the State of Ohio.

Mandatory Training

State employees, regardless of classification, who perform duties of law enforcement officers, juvenile justice and adult corrections professionals (e.g., case managers), first responders, inspectors, or investigators, shall receive two (2) hours of mandatory Anti-Human Trafficking training.

Any newly hired employees performing any of the duties listed above shall receive two (2) hours of mandatory Anti-Human Trafficking training within six (6) months of the employee’s date of hire. The training shall be incorporated into any current in-service training required for all state employees performing the duties identified above.

The training shall be provided by the Ohio Department of Public Safety (ODPS) and the Task Force and shall include, but not be limited to: overview of Human Trafficking, legal definition, the nature and scope of Human Trafficking in Ohio, vulnerabilities to victimization, methods of recruitment and control, sample cases of sex and labor Trafficking, tips for identifying potential cases of Human Trafficking, protocol upon identification, and overview of resources for follow-up training and technical assistance.

Employees who have taken Anti-Human Trafficking training through the Ohio Peace Officer Training Academy (OPOTA) will be considered to have met the requirement for the two (2) hour training and do not have to take the training offered by ODPS and the Task Force.

Agencies may be exempt from the ODPS and Task Force training if they already require Task Force-approved training for employees. See Section VI below for a link to where Agencies can find more information about how to seek Task Force approval for such training.

Elective Training

All state employees shall be offered the opportunity to voluntarily take Anti-Human Trafficking training available through web-based training provided by the Ohio Department of Administrative Services (DAS). Individual Agency directors may opt to make this training mandatory for employees of their Agency not mandated by III.A. to take the training.

Implementation

DAS’ State Human Resources Division (HRD) shall make the elective training available for all Agencies to access via DAS’ web-based training platform.

Agencies that need the two (2) hour training offered by the ODPS should contact the State Anti-Trafficking Coordinator at
sahtc@dps.ohio.gov
.

Definitions

Human Trafficking.
 The Federal Trafficking Victims Protection Act (TVPA) defines severe forms of trafficking in persons as:

Sex trafficking – the recruitment, harboring, transportation, provision, obtaining, patronizing, or soliciting of a person for the purpose of a commercial sex act, in which the commercial sex act is induced by force, fraud, or coercion or in which the person induced to perform such act has not attained 18 years of age; and/or

Labor trafficking – the recruitment, harboring, transportation, provision, or obtaining of a person for labor or services, through the use of force, fraud, or coercion for the purpose of subjection to involuntary servitude, peonage, debt bondage, or slavery.

The term “commercial sex act” means any sex act on account of which anything of value is given to or received by any person. (22 USC §7102.). In Ohio, trafficking in persons is defined in Ohio Revised Code (ORC) §2905.32. The key difference from the Federal law is that the State definition includes the element “compelled,” which is established if the state proves that the victim’s will was overcome by force, fear, duress, intimidation, or fraud.
