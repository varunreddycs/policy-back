                  Municipal Income Tax Withholding  (HR-25)


August 30, 2023

 |

Agency

State of Ohio Administrative Policy

No: HR-25 -
State Human Resources Division

Effective:
August 30, 2023

Issued By:

Kathleen C. Madden, Director

Purpose

To establish a uniform system for tax withholdings to aid State employees in meeting their obligations to pay income tax to the various municipal income taxing districts in which they work and live. The first occurrence of a defined term in the policy is in bold, italic type, and is hyperlinked to the definition in Section IV.

Scope

This policy applies to all state agencies, boards, and commissions under the authority of the Governor (collectively referred to as Agency or Agencies).

Policy

State of Ohio (State) employees are required to pay municipal income taxes on wages that are subject to such taxes. As an employer, the State of Ohio withholds and remits municipal income taxes owed on the wages of State employees that are subject to a municipal income tax. To perform this withholding function, all State employees are required to follow the standards established in this policy.

Employee Wages Subject to Municipal Income Tax Withholding

In general, a State employee’s wages may be subject to municipal income taxes based upon where the employee worked to earn those wages and/or where the State employee resides.

Work Location: For employee wages that are subject to a municipal income tax because the employee worked in a taxing municipality to earn those wages, the State of Ohio will first withhold and remit the amount owed based on the full percentage-of-tax levied by the taxing municipality where the employee worked in accordance with Section III.B. below.

Residential: For employee wages that are subject to municipal income tax because the employee resides in a municipality that applies an income tax to its residents, the State will withhold and remit the amount owed to the employee’s home municipality.

However, the residential tax rate will take into account any credits or offsets to which the employee is entitled to as a result of having municipal income taxes withheld based on the employee’s work location.

General Employee Responsibilities:

Employees are responsible for making any applicable tax filings, including residential income tax filings, and paying any taxes or seeking any refunds that may be necessitated based on the actual hours worked at each work location.

Employees must submit a Municipal Income Tax Withholding Form to their Agency payroll officer. Please see section VI below for the location of this form. Employees in Agencies that have developed their own form should use the Agency-specific form. All information provided by the employee on the Municipal Income Tax Withholding Form must be accurate and complete. Whenever an employee’s circumstances change in such a manner that the information previously provided by the employee is no longer accurate/complete, the employee must promptly submit an updated form to their Agency payroll officer.

Employees should direct any individual tax questions to their personal tax specialist.

Withholding Based on Employee Work Location

The work performed by State employees varies significantly; as such, some State employees work from a single location, while others are required to perform their job duties in multiple locations within the State. Unless otherwise required by this policy, or applicable law, the State will withhold and remit municipal income taxes based on an employee’s
Primary Work Location
.

OAKS and Kronos Users:

Both OAKS and Kronos systems (collectively referred to as Automated Timekeeping Systems) contain functionality that allows users to record their work location(s) each day.
[1]
 All employees using an Automated Timekeeping System are assigned a
Default Work Location
 for municipal income tax purposes. Typically, the Primary Work Location should be used as the Default Work Location for these employees. Municipal income taxes will be withheld using the Default Work Location unless the employee selects a different work location when recording his/her time. Employees using an Automated Timekeeping System are not required to adjust the work location field on their timesheets until the 20-day rule becomes effective as described below.

20-Day Rule: All employees—regardless of whether they ordinarily work from a single location or multiple locations—who use an Automated Timekeeping System to record their time must record their daily work location in accordance with this rule.

20 Days or Less at a Work Location: In the work location field of an employee’s time sheet, the employee can use his/her Primary Work Location for:

hours worked at that Primary Work Location; and

hours worked at a different location, but only for the first 20 days that the employee works at that different location throughout the calendar year.

More than 20 Days at a Work Location: If an employee works for 20 days
[2]
 in a calendar year at a location that differs from his/her Primary Work Location, the employee must ensure that the work location field on his/her timesheet corresponds to the different work location for any additional time worked at that different work location for the remainder of the calendar year. In other words, beginning on the 21st day that an employee works from a different location, the employee must start recording that different location whenever the employee works from there, as set forth in Section III.B.3 below.

Employees with One Work Location:

For employees who work from a single work location (e.g., a correction officer working at only one institution) and that work location is in a taxing municipality, the State will typically withhold and remit municipal income taxes owed to the municipality that contains the single work location.

OAKS and Kronos Users with One Work Location:

For Automated Timekeeping System users, their one work location is their Primary Work Location and it should be set as their Default Work Location within the system. As such, these employees typically will not have to adjust the work location field in their timesheets because their Default Work Location will automatically appear.

20-Day Rule: However, if an employee who is an Automated Timekeeping System user does work from a different location for more than 20 days in a calendar year, the employee must follow the process described in Section III.B.3.b below.

Employees with Multiple Work Locations:

For employees with multiple work locations—such as, employees with Hybrid arrangements—the transient nature of their work assignments can make them subject to municipal income taxes in more than one municipal income taxing district. To ensure that the State has accurate information for withholding purposes, there are two primary methods to record work location information:

use of an Automated Timekeeping System; or

withholding based on pre-established schedules.

As indicated earlier, Automated Timekeeping Systems may not be available to all employees. Employees must use the method selected by their Agency or their supervisor.

Method 1 – Automated Timekeeping Systems:

Subject to the 20-day rule discussed above in Section III.B.1.b, employees who use an Automated Timekeeping System to record their work hours are responsible for accurately reporting the corresponding location(s) where they worked during those hours.

Employees who use an Automated Timekeeping System should check to ensure that their Primary Work Location is used as their Default Work Location. Unless the employee’s Agency has established a different process, employees must use the Municipality Tax Withholding Form to request any change to the Default Work Location.

Method 2 – Withholding According to Pre-established Schedules:

Employees who work in multiple locations, but do not use an Automated Timekeeping System, must work with their Agency payroll officer to establish their withholding as either Type 1 or Type 2, as explained below.

Type 1: This type is reserved for employees who work a standard, pre-established percentage of their time at their Primary Work Location and a standard, pre-established percentage(s) of their time at another (other) set location(s).

Type 2: This type is reserved for employees who work at various locations without a standard, pre-established work location schedule. All hours worked for Type 2 employees will be attributed to the employee’s
Headquarter Location
.

Leave

Taxes will be withheld according to the Default Work Location.

Appointing Authority Responsibility

Appointing Authorities are responsible for determining any Agency-specific deadlines or internal processes or procedures that may be necessary in order to come into compliance with this policy. Appointing Authorities should also ensure that all employees receive notification of this policy so that employees are put on notice of their potential municipal income tax withholding obligations.

Failure to Comply with Policy

The failure of a State employee to make a good faith effort to comply with this policy will subject the employee to discipline, up to and including termination.

Headquarter County

Changes to an employee’s municipal income tax location shall not be deemed to alter an employee’s
Headquarter County
.

Definitions

Default Work Location:
 Means the default municipal income tax authority set by the appointing authority in OAKS or Kronos. The Default Work Location will pre-populate the “Work Location” field on employee timesheets. If an employee does not select a different location from the drop-down in the “Work Location” field on his or her timesheet, the State will withhold municipal income taxes according to the default location. Employees should ensure that their Primary Work Location is also used as their Default Work Location.

Headquarter County:
 Means the county which the appointing authority has determined and indicated, on the position description for each position or by other appropriate means, to be the Headquarter County. The determination shall be based on the status of the position as the primary location where the employee assigned to the position receives direction, performs the substantial duties of his/her position, or where the employee's workflow is centered.

Headquarter Location:
 For employees whose municipal income taxes are withheld according to Method 2, Type 2 (see Section III.B.3.c.iii), the Headquarter Location is typically the main location where the employee receives direction, performs the substantial duties of his/her position, or where the employee's workflow is centered.

Primary Work Location:
 Means the location at which the employee physically spends the greatest number of days in a calendar year performing services for or on behalf of the appointing authority. For example, an employee with a hybrid telework arrangement who works 60% of her/his time at Location A and 40% at location B would have a Primary Work Location of Location A. Every employee has a Primary Work Location.

Timekeeping and Tax Municipalities 
Frequently Asked Questions & Answers 
 
TIMEKEEPING SYSTEM QUESTIONS 
 
1. Where can I find job aids to assist with designating a work location in the timekeeping system? 
Job Aids are available in the myOhio Help Directory, myTax Job Aids: 
• From the myOhio homepage, click the four-square icon  at the top right corner of the page 
• Under the Help category, click Help Directory 
• Locate the desired Job Aid and click the Job Aid title 
• To open the Job Aid, click the Download button in the top right corner of the page 
 
2. Can I designate a work location if I enter time directly into OAKS? 
Yes, this functionality is available in certain versions of Kronos and OAKS. 
 
3. Will my tax information be backdated since Kronos functionality was not available January 1 through February 
13? 
No, tax information is processed (paid out) each pay period. If you believe incorrect taxes were withheld, 
contact a tax specialist for advice on the correct steps to take to request a refund. 
 
4. How do I record my time if I live in a non-taxing locality? 
Non-taxing localities are identified in Kronos and OAKS as five zeroes - “00000.” 
 
5. Do I need to designate a tax locality when entering leave? 
No, when an employee uses leave it will automatically withhold from their default work location. Employees are 
responsible for ensuring the default work location for municipal income tax liability reflects the employee’s 
primary work location. Employees should work with their agency HR/Payroll office to make changes to their 
default work location, if applicable.  
 
6. How are holidays paid out? 
Holidays automatically withhold from your default work location. Employees are responsible for ensuring the 
default work location for municipal income tax liability reflects the employee’s primary work location. 
Employees should work with their agency HR/Payroll office to make changes to their default work location, if 
applicable. 
 
7. Do I need to enter separate tax locations for the same working day? 
No, an employee should only record one work location per day on his/her timesheet. To determine to 
appropriate work location to record on your timesheet, please use the “Where did I Work?” flowchart. The 
flowchart is being finalized and will be added to these FAQs when it is available.

IMPORTANT NOTE: For employees who work from multiple locations on a single day, there is a distinction 
between the work location that you record on your timesheet and the work location that the employee will 
need to manually count/track for purposes of the 20-day rule.  For example—in a circumstance where an 
employee hasn’t worked 20 days from his/her alternate work location—if the employee works most of the day 
from an alternate work location and a small part of the day from the primary work location, the employee would 
still record the primary work location on the timesheet, but manually count (i.e., outside of the timesheet) the 
day towards the 20-day threshold of the 20-day rule.  The “Policy Questions” section below contains additional 
discussion on this point. 
 
POLICY QUESTIONS 
 
8. Where can I find the updated Policy? 
Municipal Income Tax Withholding Policy (HR-25) 
 
9. Am I required to designate a work location when entering hours worked each day in a timekeeping system? 
Employees who are subject to a municipal income tax because of their work location are required to authorize 
their appointing authority to withhold the full municipal income tax percentage applicable to that particular 
work location.  Many employees are now able to designate their work location on a daily basis in the 
timekeeping system from a drop-down menu.  Municipal income taxes will be withheld from the employee’s 
default work location for any hours that an employee does not designate the work location in the timekeeping 
system. Employees working at a single work location (e.g., a correction officer working at only one institution) 
will generally have no need to designate their work location. 
 
10. What is the “20-day rule?” 
The 20-day rule is provided for in statute and generally indicates that once an employee has reached the 21st 
day of work, during the calendar year, at a location other than their primary work location (i.e., an alternate 
work location), an employee will start owing municipal income tax to that alternate location. An employee who 
has at least one alternate work location will need to manually track (i.e., outside of the timesheet) days that 
he/she works (as explained below in Question 13) from each alternate work location. However, once an 
employee has worked 20 days (during the calendar year) from an alternate work location, the employee will no 
longer need to manually track subsequent workdays from this alternate work location for the remainder of the 
calendar year.  Rather, the employee will switch over to using his/her timesheet to record any subsequent days 
(i.e., day 21 and after) worked from this alternate work location for the remainder of the calendar year. It is 
important to remember crediting days to an alternate work location for purposes of the 20-day rule is different 
from the determination of which work location to record on your timesheet as explained above in Question 7 
and the “Where did I Work?” flowchart. For the primary work location, taxes are withheld starting on day 1. 
Which location is considered your primary location will be based on the information contained on your 
Municipal Tax Withholding Form. 
 
11. Does the 20-day rule apply separately to each location? For example, I travel to Dublin for 22 days, and Marion 
for 25 days, and Toledo for 33 days. Do I have to count all three locations for the 20-day rule separately? Or 
once I hit the 20-day rule for the first location, does it then apply for all locations for the rest of the year?

State Human Resources Division 
Each location is treated separately for the 20-day rule; if you work 22 days in Dublin and 25 in Marion, you 
would be responsible for 2 days of Dublin (days 21-22) and 5 days for Marion (days 21-25). 
 
12. What is the difference between a Method 1 and Method 2 agency? 
Method 1 agency: agencies that have the functionality to record the location of work through an Automated 
Timekeeping System (such as Kronos and OAKS). 
Method 2 agency: Agencies that do not have the functionality to record the location of work through an 
Automated Timekeeping System. 
 
13. What is considered a “day” for purposes of the 20-day rule? 
When working from multiple locations on a single day, it can be difficult to determine which work location 
should be credited with the “day” for purposes of counting under the 20-day rule.  In general, an employee 
should credit (i.e., manually count the day towards the 20-day threshold for an alternate work location) the 
workday to the alternate work location if the employee spent the most amount of time working from the 
alternate work location on that particular day. When determining which location the employee spent the most 
time working, it is important to remember that time spent traveling is considered to be performed at the 
employee’s primary work location.   
 
AGENCY HR/PAYROLL QUESTIONS 
 
14. Are agencies required to “certify” on 2021 tax returns when an employee files for a tax refund from 
municipalities? 
Agencies are not required to do so. That decision should be made internally within an agency.  It will likely be a 
case-by-case determination.  In general, there are three options.  First, agencies may choose to certify 
information contained on the employee’s refund request where the agency is reasonably certain the 
information on the form is accurate and the agency doesn’t have concerns with the conditions of the 
certification, or any other language contained on the return/refund request form. Because different locality 
refund forms contain different certification language, we suggest that agencies review each request, including 
supporting documentation, to ensure its accuracy and seek review with the immediate supervisor if necessary. 
We also suggest that agency legal counsel is consulted if there are concerns about the certification language.  
Second, agencies may choose to provide a signed letter to the employee on agency letterhead certifying any 
information that you believe to be accurate and appropriate (e.g., the number of days that you can verify the 
employee worked from an office location).  Please note, each taxing municipality is different so we do not know 
which, if any, will accept a letter in lieu of their form.  Third, the agency can decline to certify an employee’s 
request and decline to provide a letter. 
 
15. How do employees request to change their default work location for municipal income tax withholding? 
Employees can submit a Municipal Income Tax Withholding Form to their agency Payroll/HR department. This 
form was recently modified so that employees can designate the primary work location as their residence, if 
applicable.

16. On the new municipal income tax withholding form, is the "employment address" the same as the home address 
if the employee is working from home the majority of the time? 
If the employee works in the office at all, the employee should indicate the agency address. If the employee 
never works from the office, that section can be left blank. Any employee who works the majority of time from 
home should check the box indicating home address is the primary work location. 
 
17. What should employees do if they think incorrect taxes were withheld? 
Contact a tax specialist for advice on the correct steps to take to request a refund. 
 
18. What happens if an employee accidentally enters the incorrect tax locality?  
Once payroll closes, there isn't the ability to fix it. Employees are responsible for ensuring they are accurately 
reflecting tax locality. 
 
19. If an employee lives in a municipality with reciprocity, does he/she need to utilize the transfer functionality? 
Yes.  
 
20. For employees working hybrid schedules and who have the same tax locality for their residence and for their 
office, do they need to utilize the transfer functionality? 
If the tax locality is the same for all locations worked, the employee does not need to use the transfer 
functionality.
