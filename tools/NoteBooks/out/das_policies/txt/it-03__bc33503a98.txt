                    IT-03 Software Licensing


State of Ohio Administrative Policy

No: IT-03
 - Information Technology

Effective:
 January 30, 2026

Issued By:


Kathleen C. Madden, Director

Purpose

This policy requires Agencies to establish controls for the use, management and protection of Licensed Software required to conduct official state business. All defined terms are capitalized and a link to the glossary of definitions is found in section IV.

Scope

This policy applies to all state Agencies, boards, and commissions under the authority of the Governor (collectively referred to as Agency or Agencies).

Policy

Agencies shall acquire, distribute, audit, manage, remove and dispose of Licensed Software in accordance with the requirements of this policy.

Authorized Software

Agencies shall maintain an up-to-date list of Authorized Software that is used to conduct official state business.

Software acquired for official state business shall be licensed in the name of the state or Agency. Software shall not be licensed in the name of an individual.


Agencies shall ensure that the Intellectual Property Holder grants permission to use the software and that any use of software is in compliance with associated license agreements.

Prior to entering into software agreements, Agencies shall ensure that a state contract or enterprise shared service is not already in place for the desired solution and that the product is procured in accordance with Ohio Administrative Policies
PM-01 Purchasing Goods and Services
 and
Emergency Purchasing Procedures PM-02
.

Agencies shall work with the
IT Strategic Investment & Architecture
 to ensure the necessary subject matter expert reviews and approvals are conducted prior to purchasing new software (e.g., security, privacy, DAS service owner, IT architecture).

When an Agency enters into a software license agreement that involves creating, receiving, maintaining, or transmitting state data or access to state systems, the Agency shall obtain satisfactory assurances that the contractor will appropriately safeguard the information. Those assurances shall be consistent with requirements established by the DAS Office of Procurement Services, DAS Office of Legal Services, and the DAS Office of Information Security and Privacy (OISP). The DAS Office of Procurement Services, DAS Office of Legal Services, and OISP shall develop processes to make certain that the most up-to-date security and privacy requirements are included in all competitive solicitations involving the procurement of software (e.g., requests for proposals, master cloud service agreements).

Agencies shall maintain an inventory of all Authorized Software. Licensed Software records shall be maintained in such a way as to be sufficient to determine the license type, duration, and terms and conditions associated with the software licenses. The type of information collected and maintained might include, but is not limited to, the following:


Title.

Publisher.

License type.

Initial installation/use date.

Purchase documentation.

Actual license agreement.

Location of each installation/use of the Licensed Software.

Business purpose.

Support mechanism(s) (e.g., patching tool, manual patches, etc.).

Where appropriate, also include Uniform Resource Locator (URL), App store(s), Version(s), Deployment mechanism, and Decommission date.

			At a minimum, the software inventory shall be reviewed and updated, if necessary, bi-annually. When possible, Agencies shall automate the detection and recording of installed software through inventory management tools. The software inventory shall also be maintained in accordance with
Ohio IT Standard ITS-SEC-02, “Enterprise Security Controls Framework
.”

Agencies shall strive to use only Supported Software. which means the software must receive Patches and Firmware updates, have appropriate security assurances, active maintenance contracts in place with agreed upon expiration dates, and align with state and Agency policies and standards. If software is unsupported, yet deemed essential to support Agency business, the Agency shall document an exception. The exception shall detail the security and privacy controls that are in place to protect against any identified Risks. The Agency shall also document their acknowledgment and acceptance of any remaining Risk.


Unsupported software shall only be installed where necessary (i.e., essential to support Agency business) and shall be identified and tracked within the software inventory.

Unsupported software installations shall be removed when no longer needed.

Even if software is developed in-house, there may be components that are governed by the terms and conditions of an external party. Agencies shall ensure that software licensing agreements are followed in instances of internally developed software. Agencies shall understand and align with the requirements of software development licenses, the terms and conditions may be different from standard licenses.

Agencies shall assess and understand the Risk associated with using open source software source code. In addition, Agencies shall develop processes to ensure that the use of open source software source code is reviewed and approved prior to use. Agencies shall conduct security tests, including source code reviews, for design flaws and common security coding errors (e.g., Buffer Overflow, Race Condition, Command Injection, Cross-site Scripting).

Software Installation/Use

Agencies shall establish installation/use procedures based upon this policy and the software use requirements outlined in
Ohio Administrative Policy IT-04, “Use of Internet, E-mail, and Other IT Resources
.” The procedures shall validate that software is authorized, properly licensed, supported and that the staff authorized to manage it is identified. These procedures shall also determine whether the Agency will permit installation of state-licensed software on privately owned computers.

Installation of state-licensed software on privately owned computers is generally discouraged. If Agencies permit installation of state-licensed software on privately owned computers, there shall be an authorizing document that requires adherence to Agency software licensing policies, information technology security policies and acceptable use policies as well as to the terms of software license agreements. Agencies shall also ensure the software license agreements allow the practice.

Software License Management

Agencies shall develop software license management procedures that as a minimum address the following:

Evaluation and selection of the most cost-effective licensing model.

An authorization process, which includes an evaluation of the vendor’s security and privacy practices, a review of software license terms and conditions by appropriate Agency personnel (e.g., legal, security), and demonstration of a valid or continued business need.

Monitoring of vendor licensing practices to address any planned changes in licensing models.

Migration plans for Licensed Software that is nearing or has reached the end of support.

Removal and/or reassignment of software licenses in the case of abrupt termination or resignation of employees, contractors, temporary personnel or other agents of the state. Agencies shall ensure that when the use of privately owned computers is authorized, removal procedures are in place for instances of transfer, resignation or abrupt termination
/resignation.

Software Audit

Agencies shall establish procedures for conducting periodic Licensed Software audits. The audits shall ensure:

That Agencies verify only Authorized Software is being used. Audit procedures shall compare the terms and conditions of the license agreements against actual usage. If discrepancies are found, corrective action shall be taken to align Agency usage with the terms and conditions of the software agreement. Any unauthorized software shall be removed from use. If for some reason the software cannot be removed, an exception shall be documented that includes acknowledgment of the Risk and defines mitigation controls.

Whether Authorized Software users are still employed at the Agency and still require access to assigned software. If the licensee is no longer employed or no longer requires access, the software licenses shall be terminated or transferred to another user, if appropriate.

Software Disposal

Agencies shall comply with
Ohio Administrative Policy IT-05, “Disposal, Servicing and Transfer of IT Equipment
,” and software license agreements to allow for the proper disposition of Licensed Software and associated media. Information technology equipment that is recycled or salvaged shall be processed as necessary to comply with the terms of license agreements and to prevent unauthorized use of Licensed Software.

Education and Awareness

Agencies shall incorporate software licensing concepts into education and awareness programs.

Agencies shall implement a management training program covering its software licensing practices to ensure that managers and supervisors understand how to comply with this state information technology policy and Agency procedures for the use of Licensed Software. Training may include items such as:


How the Agency purchases software.

Software inventory requirements.

Software audit procedures.

Software disposal.

Agencies shall develop education and awareness programs to inform employees, contractors, temporary personnel and other agents of the state of the applicable requirements of this state information technology policy and Agency procedures for the use of Licensed Software. Training shall be provided during initial orientation and as part of an ongoing education and awareness program addressing items such as:


Authorized Software.

Personal use of Agency software.

Software installation procedures.

Applicable laws, regulations and state policies, including state and individual liabilities.

Definitions

All defined terms are capitalized within the policy and the online glossary of definitions can be found at
IT Policy Glossary - Department of Administrative Services
.
