 |

Agency

State of Ohio Administrative Policy

No: HR-10 -
State Human Resources Division

Effective:
January 11, 2026

Issued By:
Kathleen C. Madden, Director

Purpose

To provide uniform guidelines and procedures for the implementation of section 124.388 of the Ohio Revised Code (ORC). The first occurrence of a defined term in the policy is in bold, italic type, and is hyperlinked to the definition in Section IV.

Scope

This policy applies to all state agencies, boards, and commissions under the authority of the Governor (collectively referred to as Agency or Agencies).

Policy

Generally, employees are expected to use their own leave to be compensated for absences from work. However, an Appointing Authority may, in its discretion, place an employee on administrative leave (with or without pay), pursuant to ORC 124.388. Agencies must consult their legal counsel prior to placing an employee on administrative leave with or without pay. Each situation will differ and requires a case-by-case determination, including a review of any applicable collective bargaining agreement.

Criteria for Using Administrative Leave

Agencies may use administrative leave (with or without pay) within their discretion. However, employees can only be placed on administrative leave when the specific criteria/circumstances in ORC 124.388 apply and as further explained below.

Administrative leave with pay may be used in the following circumstances:

When the health or safety of an employee or of any person or property entrusted to the employee’s care could be adversely affected.

If the circumstances necessitate immediate action to remove an employee(s) from the workplace because of a threat to the health and/or safety of an employee(s) such as a fire, active aggressor incident, or other need for an immediate evacuation of the worksite, administrative leave may be used. Note, administrative leave is not required in these or other similar situations. Further, other options such as teleworking may be appropriate.

If there is a threat that is not immediate to any individual or group of individuals or property entrusted to an employee, administrative leave may be appropriate to alleviate the threat. Agencies should consider whether a modification of duties or a temporary reassignment to another work area, shift, or position/vacancy (as long as the employee meets the minimum qualifications) may alleviate the threat thereby avoiding the need to place an individual on administrative leave with pay. Agencies must consult applicable collective bargaining agreements when considering these options for bargaining unit employees.

In cases where no other options are available to avoid adverse impact to the health and safety of persons or property, it is permissible to place an employee on administrative leave with pay pending a review, independent medical examination, investigation, or disciplinary proceeding surrounding the situation. In instances where an internal administrative investigation is being conducted, administrative leave with pay should generally be authorized for the period of time necessary to conduct the investigation and shall include the period of time needed to decide whether to impose discipline. If an independent criminal review or investigation arises, the employee may remain on administrative leave with pay pending criminal investigation and/or legal proceedings.

When an employee is transferred in accordance with ORC 124.33, the Agency may grant up to two days of administrative leave with pay to the employee.

Administrative leave without pay may only be used when an employee has been charged with a violation of a law that is punishable as a felony. Administrative leave without pay cannot exceed two months and if an employee does not plead guilty to the felony charge or if the employee is not found guilty of a felony, the Agency shall repay the employee at the employee’s base rate of pay, plus interest at the statutory rate set forth in ORC 5703.47 for the period of time the employee was on unpaid administrative leave.

Length of Administrative Leave

The Agency shall be responsible for monitoring the length of time that an employee is on administrative leave.

The length of administrative leave with pay is at the discretion of the Agency but should be consistent with the guidance in Section III.A. and based on the individual circumstances. Agencies should continually analyze and review the situation to determine if administrative leave with pay is still supported by the criteria set forth in ORC 124.388 and Section III.A.1. of this policy.

An Agency may place an employee on administrative leave without pay for up to two months, as provided in Section III.A.2 of this policy. The administrative leave without pay cannot exceed the two-month period. Agencies must conduct an analysis to determine what leave, if any, is appropriate under the circumstances if the employee will not be returning to work following a period of administrative leave without pay.

Employee Notification

Employees placed on administrative leave (either with pay or without pay) for more than one workday shall be notified in writing at the outset of the leave. The notice should state whether the leave is with or without pay. Employees should also be notified of the following while they are on administrative leave:

If the administrative leave is paid, the employee shall be in a work-ready status at all times during their regular scheduled work hours, and must be able to report to work with short notice.

The employee shall not enter the Agency’s property during the leave unless required by the Agency.

The employee shall call the Agency prior to making arrangements to conduct business or to otherwise be on the Agency’s property.

The employee shall make arrangements with the Agency for issuing of paychecks, the processing of vacation, personal and other leave requests, as well as other personnel or employee benefits matters.

If the administrative leave is unpaid, possible arrangements for employee to directly pay for insurance premiums during the administrative leave.

If the administrative leave is paid, the employee shall not hold another job during the hours they are normally scheduled to work. If employees work during such hours, they will forfeit the administrative leave with pay and shall be subject to discipline.
