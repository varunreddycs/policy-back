I. Purpose 
This policy, in conjunction with Ohio IT Standard ITS -SEC-01/Data Encryption and Cryptography  
(ITS-SEC-01), provides guidance to Agencies  as they take steps to protect Confidential Data and 
Information. Additionally, this policy addresses Agency Encryption practices for the security of 
Records that are potentially subject to an investigatory effort. The first occurrence of a defined 
term in the policy is in bold, italic type, and is hyperlinked to the definition in Section IV. 
II. Scope 
This policy applies to all Agencies , boards, and commissions under the authority of the Governor 
(collectively referred to as Agency or Agencies). 
III. Policy 
It is critical that Agencies implement the appropriate safeguards to protect Confidential Data and 
Information. This policy outlines the requirements for identifying and securing Confidential  Data 
as well as the Portable Devices and Media on which Confidential Data resides. 
 
A. Identify and Label Confidential Data: Agencies must classify Data, systems, Media, Portable 
Devices and electronic transmissions  and establish procedures to identify, label and secure 
Data in accordance with Ohio Administrative Policy IT -13/Data Classification, and the 
accompanying Ohio IT Standard ITS-SEC-04/Data Classification.  
 
B. Use Only State-Approved Strong Encryption: Any use of Encryption to protect Confidential 
Data must conform to ITS -SEC-01. Agencies must ensure that a cryptographic key 
management plan is in place that protects the creation, distribution and storage of

C. Secure Confidential Data in Transmission : Agencies must secure Confidential Data in 
transmission. Whenever Confidential Data travels over the Internet or other untrusted 
channels, as a minimum, Encryption must be used to safeguard the Data. 
 
1. In particular, the following forms of transmission over untrusted channels must  be 
Encrypted:  
a) e-mail;  
b) pages on state-controlled websites;  
c) instant messaging; 
d) remote printing; 
e) Data transfers; 
f) copying of Data to removable Media; and  
g) any wireless transmission.  
2. Agencies must establish a process to check Data in transmission for activities that risk 
unauthorized access to or disclosure of Confidential Data. There are different means of 
checking for these types of activities,  and they include increased activity logging, spot 
audits, the use of content monitoring, and lexical analysis tools among others. 
 
D. Secure Confidential Data at Rest: Agencies must secure Confidential Data at rest. Regardless 
of whether access is via trusted or untrusted channels, Agencies  must provide strong Access 
Controls for Confidential Data at rest. As a minimum, Agencies must protect Confidential Data 
at rest through Encryption. The following Agency practices must be in place to secure access 
to Confidential Data systems: 
 
1. Implement restrictions on the downloading of Confidential Data.  
2. Apply authorization controls so that individual access is limited to a need-to-know basis 
based on the User's role in the Agency.  
3. Employ a session lock for accounts, including those initiated via remote access or 
Portable Device s, that requires re -authentication after no more than 30 minutes of 
inactivity.  
4. Implement a process for prompt deactivation of accounts for User s who are no longer 
employed, must no longer have access, or are subject to an action requiring deactivation. 
5. Conduct a regular validation of User accounts to ensure that access rights are assigned, 
as appropriate.

E. Secure Backups: In performing Confidential Data backups and restorations, Agencies must 
ensure:  
1. Encryption is consistently applied to backup Portable Devices, Media and active Data. 
2. Data backups enforce the most current Access Controls.  
3. Reuse of backup Media  is limited to the same set of Confidential  Data or is securely 
sanitized in accordance with NIST SP 800 -88, "Guidelines for Media  Sanitization," (NIST 
SP 800-88) if it is used for another purpose. 
4. Backup Media is destroyed in accordance with NIST SP 800 -88 guidelines once it is no 
longer necessary. 
5. Appropriate physical security controls must be in place, including:  
a) Physical access to backups of Confidential Data must be limited to authorized 
personnel only.  
b) Physical transportation of backup Media must be secure.  
(i) Transport must be provided by a state employee or state -approved secure 
carrier. 
(ii) When possible, backup Media must be transported using a locked, tamper proof 
box to secure the Media. 
c) Physical storage of Confidential Data backups and restorations must be located at a 
state-owned or state-approved, secure facility.  
 
F. Secure Confidential Data on Portable Devices and Media : At a minimum, Agencies must 
implement the following controls for the placement of Confidential Data on Portable Devices 
and Media:  
 
1. A procedure must be developed for authorizing the placement of Confidential Data onto 
Portable Devices and Media. The procedure must include the following: 
a) A required Risk Assessment that will assist in determining the level of Risk 
associated with the use of a given Portable Device or type of Media.  
b) Written authorization for the placement of Confidential  Data on Portable Devices 
and Media. 
c) Written acknowledgement from each User  that he/she agrees to comply with the 
security requirements of this policy, state IT security policies and Agency IT security 
policies. 
2. The use of Encryption and strong passwords must be used to protect Confidential Data 
on Portable Devices and Media. Encryption may be employed at the Data-level, file-level, 
or operating system. 
3. A procedure must  be developed for the removal or destruction of Confidential  Data on 
Portable Devices and Media  that aligns with NIST SP 800- 88, and the requirements

G. Physically Secure Confidential  Data: Agencies must secure the physical Portable D evices, 
locations, and facilities used for Confidential Data processing and storage.  
 
1. Only authorized personnel must  be allowed to access or remove Portable D evices and 
Media containing Confidential Data.  
2. Under no circumstances must unencrypted Confidential Data be stored or transported in 
a manner that is not physically secure. For unencrypted Confidential  Data, "physically 
secure" means implementing multiple layers of physical security that use facilities and 
services designed for securing high Risk Data and certifying that the facilities or services 
take the necessary physical security precautions.  
 
H. Communicate Expectations for Handling Confidential Data: Agencies must ensure that 
Users are aware of all of the requirements associated with the protection of Confidential Data 
and that they actively acknowledge their role. Agencies must require Users to agree in writing 
to take precautions to protect Confidential  Data. The written agreement must  include the 
following:  
 
1. Everyone has a duty to protect Confidential Data.  
2. Confidential Data must not be disclosed without authorization.  
3. Access to Confidential Data must not be provided without proper authorization.  
4. Confidential Data must not be stored on Portable Devices that are personally-owned or 
otherwise not controlled by the state.  
5. There is no expectation of privacy when using state Portable Devices. The state has the 
right to access, inspect, and monitor any state Portable D evice or service, including any 
files on or communications through state Portable Devices or services. 
6. The penalties associated with violating the agreement.  
 
I. Be Prepared to Respond to a n Information Security and Privacy Incident : Agencies must 
develop incident response procedures that specifically address a compromise or exposure of 
Confidential Data and align with Ohio Administrative Policy IT -18/Information Security & 
Privacy Incident Response. Agency incident response procedures must include a plan to notify 
and respond to the persons affected.

J. Investigations and Data Encryption : In the context of an investigation, Agencies  must 
provide access to Encrypted Information to law enforcement or other investigatory agents 
upon a legally authorized request for a search or electronic monitoring. Agencies  must 
establish procedures to support the following actions prior to and upon presentation of a 
request to access Encrypted Information. 
 
1. Only Use Encryption Solutions that Permit Key Recovery: Except in situations where 
Encryption is used to Tokenize  or Anonymize Data (e.g., Data analytics), Agencies must 
maintain cryptographic keys in such a manner that the investigatory Agency  may 
confidentially access any Encrypted Data. The solution must contain a key management 
component that allows for a highly restricted secondary means of access to E ncrypted 
Information.  
2. Develop and Document Secure Encryption and Encryption Key Management Practices: 
Agencies must develop Encryption practices consistent with State of Ohio IT policies and 
standards, including IT Standard ITS-SEC-01. Both physical and logical controls must be 
used to secure cryptographic keys and access to keys must  be logged. The compromise 
of a system storing cryptographic keys can potentially compromise all cryptographic 
protections used in an Agency. Therefore, such systems must be treated as higher-impact 
targets. To ensure access to E ncrypted Records is possible, Agencies must maintain 
cryptographic keys for Records in accordance with the relevant retention schedule of the 
Record being Encrypted. Agencies must thoroughly document their Encryption practices 
and secure this documentation accordingly. Agencies must also ensure that backup and 
recovery of cryptographic keys are a part of the Agency's Continuity of Operations Plan. 
3. Designate Encryption Points -of-Contact: The Agency  chief Information officer, or 
designee, must have access to cryptographic keys and must serve as an Encryption point-
of-contact. In addition, the Agency  must appoint one or more Encryption points-of-
contact who must have access to cryptographic keys. Agencies must subject all personnel 
with access to system assets to a vetting process commensurate with the system assets' 
Risk Assessment. Personnel with access to cryptographic keys or Confidential  
Information require thorough vetting. 
4. Identify and Authenticate the Investigator and the Request: The Agency Encryption point-
of-contact must consult Agency legal counsel to validate requests for Record s or 
Information, including subpoenas, warrants, and investigatory requests.  
a) A request, subpoena, or warrant to access a computer, removable Media, or other IT 
asset includes by definition the means to access all Information  on the IT asset, 
including Encrypted Information. If the Agency chief information officer is the 
subject of the investigation, the investigatory Agency  must forward the request to 
another Agency Encryption point-of-contact.

IV. Definitions 
 
A. Access Controls. Process of granting or denying specific requests: 1) for obtaining and using 
Information and related I nformation processing services; and 2) to enter specific physical 
facilities.1 
B. Anonymize. A process that removes the association between the identifying Data set and the 
Data subject.2 
C. Availability. Ensuring timely and reliable access to and use of information.3 
D. Confidential Data. This classification includes Data that is required to be protected by law or 
regulation, is intended for confidential use, and may not be copied or removed from an 
agency's operational control without authorized permission.  This classification includes Data 
that, if compromised, may result in loss of life, serious injury, or other harm to an individual 
or group, or disruption to critical agency operations. 
E. Confidentiality. Preserving authorized restrictions on information access and disclosure, 
including means for protecting personal privacy and proprietary information.4 
F. Continuity of Operations Plan (COOP) . The documentation of a predetermined set of 
instructions or procedures that describe how an organization’s Mission Essential Functions 
will be sustained during and after a significant disruption. 5 The COOP is also commonly 
referred to as a business continuity plan. 
G. Data. Coded representation of quantities, objects and actions. The word, “Data,” is often used 
interchangeably with the word, “Information.”

H. D enial -of-Service (DoS). The prevention of authorized access to resources or the delaying of 
time-critical operations. (Time -critical may be milliseconds or it may be hours, depending 
upon the service provided.)6 
I. Encryption. The use of an algorithmic process to transform Data into a form in which there is 
a low probability of assigning meaning without use of a confidential process or key. 
J. Information. Data processed into a form that has meaning and value to the recipient to 
support an action or decision. “Information” is often used interchangeably with “Data.” 
K. Information Security and Privacy Incident (Security Incident) . An occurrence that actually or 
potentially jeopardizes the Confidentiality , Integrity or Availability of state I nformation 
resources. Privacy incidents are  a subset of Security Incidents. Examples of Security Incidents 
include Ransomware, exfiltration of Data, Denial -of-Service, account compromise, loss or 
theft of a computing or Data storage device, improper usage or access, Information Spillage, 
and successful Social Engineering. 
L. Information Spillage . Instances where information is placed on systems that are not 
authorized to process such information. Information spills occur when information that is 
thought to be a certain classification or impact level is transmitted to a system and 
subsequently is determined to be of a higher classification or impact level.7  
M. Integrity. Guarding against improper information modification or destruction and includes 
ensuring information non-repudiation and authenticity.8 
N. Media. Any device that is capable of storing Information . Media is not required to be capable 
of processing Information. This definition includes, but is not limited to, the following: 
1. external/removable hard drives; 
2. magnetic tapes; 
3. Portable Devices; 
4. optical Media such as compact disks (CDs), digital video disks (DVDs), etc.; and 
5. thumb drives (USB keys). 
O. Portable Devices. Computer or device designed for mobile use. A Portable Device includes 
laptops, smartphones or tablets. 
P. Records. As defined in Section 149.011 of the Ohio Revised Code (ORC). 
Q. Risk. A measure of the extent to which an entity is threatened by a potential circumstance or 
event, and typically a function of: (1) the adverse impacts that would arise if the circumstance
