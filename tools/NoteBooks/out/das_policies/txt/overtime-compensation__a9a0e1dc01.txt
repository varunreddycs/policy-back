                  Overtime Compensation (HR-06)


August 31, 2023

 |

Agency

State of Ohio Administrative Policy

No: HR-06 -
State Human Resources Division

Effective:
August 31, 2023

Issued By:
Kathleen C. Madden, Director

Purpose

To require all agencies to adopt a uniform overtime compensation policy that complies with the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), Ohio Revised Code (ORC) 124.18, and Chapter 123:1-43 of the Ohio Administrative Code (OAC). The first occurrence of a defined term in the policy is in bold, italic type, and is hyperlinked to the definition in Section IV.

Scope

This policy applies to all state agencies, boards, and commissions under the authority of the Governor (collectively referred to as Agencies or Agency), and to employees who are exempt from collective bargaining and overtime eligible pursuant to ORC 124.18 and the FLSA or who are overtime exempt pursuant to the FLSA but whose Agency has requested and received approval from the Ohio Department of Administrative Services (DAS) to pay overtime compensation in accordance with ORC 124.18(A) (see Section III.D. of this policy). For requirements related to overtime compensation for bargaining unit employees, refer to the applicable collective bargaining agreement.

Policy

Agencies are required to compensate eligible employees for overtime hours worked in a fair and consistent manner, and in compliance with applicable laws. Any deviation from this policy must be requested and approved by the Director of DAS prior to implementation.

Overtime Designations

Agencies must ensure that they have properly designated positions as overtime exempt per the criteria established by the FLSA. All overtime exempt designations must be tracked in accordance with this policy. Guidelines are attached to this policy to assist Agencies in properly designating positions (see
Attachment 1 - Guidelines for Reviewing Overtime Exempt Designations
). Agency human resources staff should consult with Agency legal counsel with respect to assigning appropriate designations at the position level. Additionally, Agencies:

Must ensure the appropriate overtime designation has been made in OAKS;

Must review the current FLSA designation and indicate the appropriate designation on the position description as existing position descriptions are updated or new position descriptions are created; and

Are encouraged to work with Agency legal counsel to establish a regular audit timetable to ensure that employees are appropriately designated and continue to perform duties consistent with their designation.

Overtime Compensation for Overtime Eligible Employees

Earning Overtime: An overtime eligible employee who is in an active pay status for more than 40 hours in a work week is entitled to overtime compensation or compensatory time. Active pay status does not include sick leave or leave used in lieu of sick leave.

An employee entitled to overtime compensation shall be compensated for such time over 40 hours at one and one-half times his or her base rate of pay. An employee may elect to earn compensatory time in lieu of overtime pay for such time over 40 hours on a time and one-half basis (see Section III.C.).

Regardless of the number of hours worked on any individual day, an overtime eligible employee is not entitled to compensation for overtime work unless he or she is in an active pay status for more than 40 hours in a work week, or as otherwise proscribed under the ORC or FLSA.

Overtime compensation should only be earned by overtime-eligible employees for work hours that are authorized as immediately necessary to the operation of their office.

Overtime Approval and Documentation: Before exceeding 40 hours in active pay status in any work week, an overtime eligible employee must have the overtime work approved in advance pursuant to Agency policy and procedure. No overtime eligible employee shall begin work prior to his or her scheduled work hours or continue to work after his or her scheduled work hours or during a scheduled lunch period or other unpaid meal break, without prior approval. Agency policy or procedure should address what employees should do in an unforeseen situation when no one is available to authorize overtime.

Overtime eligible employees shall document all hours worked, including overtime worked, in a manner determined by the Agency.

Agencies shall be responsible for monitoring overtime work, and for ensuring that all overtime worked is documented through the Agency’s timekeeping system and that the employee obtained prior approval to work the overtime.

Employees who work overtime without approval shall be compensated for the time worked but may be subject to corrective counseling or disciplinary action for failure to follow the Agency’s approval process.

Earning and Using Overtime-Eligible Compensatory Time

An overtime eligible employee may elect to earn compensatory time in lieu of overtime pay, up to a maximum of 240 hours, except that if the work of an employee for which compensatory time may be provided includes work in a public safety activity, an emergency response activity, or a seasonal activity, the employee engaged in such work may accrue not more than 480 hours of compensatory time. Such compensatory time will be earned on a time and one-half basis. When an employee has a compensatory time balance at the maximum amount, any additional overtime worked shall be paid as overtime compensation.

Compensatory time balances will be maintained in the Ohio Administrative Knowledge System (OAKS). A report of compensatory time balances may be maintained and reported on the employee’s paycheck.

Compensatory time use must be requested and documented in accordance with Agency policy. The employee may use the compensatory time at a time mutually convenient to the employee and the employee’s administrative supervisor.

Any hours of compensatory time shall be paid to the employee if:

The employee has not used the compensatory time within 365 days after it is earned;

The employee transfers to another Agency or state appointing authority;

A change in the employee’s overtime-eligibility status exempts the employee from the payment of overtime compensation; or

The employee separates from state employment, paid at the rate that is the greater of the employee’s final regular rate of pay or the employee’s average regular rate of pay during the last three years of employment with the state.

Requesting Overtime Payment to FLSA Overtime Exempt Personnel

If an Agency desires to grant overtime compensation at time and one-half or straight time or compensatory time at time and one-half pursuant to ORC 124.18(A) to state employees who are exempt under the FLSA, the Agency must submit a request to DAS and receive DAS approval prior to granting such overtime compensation. Before submitting this request to DAS, the Agency should consider whether other options will assist it in meeting its needs. Other options include allowing overtime exempt employees flextime or the opportunity to earn compensatory time on an hour for hour basis, in accordance with State of Ohio Administrative Policy HR-08. Agencies must send an official letter to DAS, signed by the director or appointing authority of the Agency, requesting approval to allow FLSA overtime exempt personnel to receive payment for overtime on a time and one-half basis or straight time basis or the earning of compensatory time at time and one-half. The request must contain:

A listing of the first and last names, classifications, State of Ohio User ID (SOUID), and Position Numbers for all exempt employees who will be eligible. This listing must be submitted electronically on an Excel spreadsheet, along with a description of the duties performed by those exempt employees;

Criteria the Agency used to select the eligible individual employees, positions, or classifications;

Specific hours, days per week, times of the year, or isolated situations when eligible employees, positions, or classifications may receive overtime or earn compensatory time at time and one-half;

Justification of the need, including an analysis of the reason(s) that alternative means are not appropriate. Issues such as salary compression or recruitment and retention are insufficient to justify the request. The request submitted for approval must show either that:

The employees perform functions essential to maintain the safety, security, and health of Ohio’s citizens, or

The employees work in a 24-hour operation and the affected employees must work overtime because of direct care, safety, or security reasons.

Statement certifying that the payment of overtime to FLSA overtime exempt employees is within an Agency’s existing payroll appropriations.

If there are any changes to the classifications and/or position numbers of exempt employees who will be eligible to receive payment for overtime under the Agency’s approved policy, the Agency must update and resubmit its request for approval. Agencies that receive approval shall track this specific overtime cost using the appropriate code provided by DAS. DAS will review these payments each pay period to ensure compliance with the approval.

Attachment 1 - Guidelines for Reviewing Overtime Exempt Designations

The following guidelines may be helpful in reviewing whether an overtime-exempt designation is appropriate under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This is only a guideline. Agency human resources staff should consult with Agency legal counsel in evaluating positions and duties under the FLSA and should also refer to applicable collective bargaining agreements.

Collective-bargaining exempt positions that are executive, administrative, professional, or highly compensated under the FLSA should generally be designated as overtime exempt.

Executive Exemption: This exemption is applicable to employees who are paid a salary of at least $684 per week or $35,568 per year and:

whose primary duty is management of the enterprise or a customarily recognized department or subdivision of the enterprise;

who customarily and regularly direct the work of two or more full-time employees or their equivalent; and

who have the authority to hire and fire, or have particular weight given to their suggestion on hiring, firing, advancement, promotion, or other changes to the status of other employees.

Administrative Exemption: This exemption is applicable to employees who are paid a salary of at least $684 per week or $35,568 per year and:

whose primary duty is the performance of office or non-manual work directly related to management or general business operations of the employer or the employer’s clients; and

whose primary duty includes the exercise of discretion and independent judgment with respect to matters of significance.

Professional Exemption: There are numerous types of professional exemptions. The most relevant ones are learned professionals and creative professionals. This exemption is applicable to employees who are paid a salary of at least $684 per week or $35,568 per year.

Learned professionals are employees:

whose primary duty is the performance of work requiring advanced knowledge, defined as work which is predominantly intellectual in character and which includes work requiring the consistent exercise of discretion and judgment;

whose advanced knowledge is in a field of science or learning; and

whose advanced knowledge is customarily acquired by a prolonged course of specialized intellectual instruction.

Creative professionals are employees: whose primary duty is the performance of work requiring invention, imagination, originality, or talent in a recognized field of artistic or creative endeavor.

The FLSA also provides an exception for teachers and employees who hold a license and engage in the practice of law or medicine, which does not require meeting the above salary requirements.

Computer Exemption: This exemption is applicable to employees who are paid a salary of at least $684 per week or $35,568 per year, or if compensated on an hourly basis, at a rate of not less than $27.63 per hour, and who are employed as a computer systems analyst, computer programmer, software engineer, or other similarly skilled worker in the computer field performing the duties described below:

The application of systems analysis techniques and procedures, including consulting with users, to determine hardware, software, or system functional specifications;

The design, development, documentation, analysis, creation, testing, or modification of computer systems or programs, including prototypes, based on and related to user or system design specifications;

The design, documentation, testing, creation, or modification of computer programs related to machine operating systems; or

A combination of the aforementioned duties, the performance of which requires the same level of skills.

Highly Compensated Exemption: This exemption is applicable to employees who earn an annual compensation of $107,432 or more, which includes at least $684 per week paid on a salary basis and

whose primary duty is the performance of office or non-manual work; and

who customarily and regularly perform at least one of the exempt duties or responsibilities of an exempt executive, administrative, or professional employee.

The actual duties of the position must be thoroughly examined before reaching a conclusion that a position is overtime exempt. Job titles are not sufficient to determine whether a position is overtime exempt under the FLSA. A simple, cursory review of a position description or job classification alone is insufficient. Position descriptions should be updated accordingly indicating FLSA exemption if applicable.

Police officers, detectives, state troopers, highway patrol officers, investigators, inspectors, correctional officers, parole or probation officers, park rangers, fire fighters, paramedics, emergency medical technicians, ambulance personnel, rescue workers, hazardous materials workers, and similar employees, regardless of rank or pay level, who perform work such as preventing, controlling, or extinguishing fires of any type; rescuing fire, crime, or accident victims; preventing or detecting crimes; conducting investigations or inspections for violations of law; performing surveillance; pursuing, restraining, and apprehending suspects; detaining or supervising suspected and convicted criminals, including those on probation or parole; interviewing witnesses; interrogating and fingerprinting suspects; preparing investigative reports; or other similar work are not overtime exempt employees under the exemptions listed above.

Although state pay schedules include hourly rates, state employees are assumed to be treated as salaried employees for the purpose of this analysis.
