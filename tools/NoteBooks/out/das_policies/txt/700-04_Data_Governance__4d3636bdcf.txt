I. Purpose 
The purpose of this policy is to outline requirements for the establishment, maintenance, and use 
of a formal Ohio Department of Administrative Services (DAS)  Data Governance framework that 
ensures appropriate decision-making and accountability in the data lifecycle, including creation 
or collection, processing, dissemination, use, storage, disposition, destruction , and deletion of 
DAS Data and Data Analytics. The first occurrence of a defined term in the policy is in bold, italic 
type, and is hyperlinked to the definition in Section IV.  
II. Scope 
This policy applies to all DAS programs, employees, and contracted individuals and to all Data that 
is created or maintained by DAS under its authority for its purposes.  
 
This policy is not intended to address public records requests, which are defined in Ohio Revised 
Code (ORC) Section 149.43 (“Ohio Public Records Law”). 
III. Policy 
It is the policy of DAS  that all D ata and D ata Products shall be protected, managed, and used 
appropriately pursuant to law, policy, and procedures  to promote the efficient and effective 
operation of state government in accordance with State of Ohio Policy IT-19, Data Governance. At 
a minimum, DAS shall include and account for the following:

A. DAS Data Governance Roles and Responsibilities 
 
1. Organizational Users must responsibly use State Data that resides within state-owned, 
managed, or governed Information Technology (IT) Resources and includes: 
a) Personally Identifiable Information  (PII) and Confidential Personal Information 
(CPI), 
b) Sensitive Data, and 
c) Data input into or generated by Artificial Intelligence (AI). 
2. The Data Owner must ensure that all Data and Data Products within their Data Domain 
are classified, protected, and managed appropriately pursuant to law, policy, and 
procedures. The Data Owner is also responsible for authorizing access and/or sharing of 
agency Data through the creation of Data use or sharing agreements, in consultation with 
their designated legal and privacy representatives. The Data Owner must serve as the 
primary point of contact for regulatory audits and must work in collaboration with Data 
Stewards and Data Custodians to provide responses. 
3. The Data Steward must provide day-to-day management of a d ataset and serve as the 
subject matter expert who understands and communicates the meaning and use of Data. 
4. The Data Custodian must provide safe custody, transport, and storage of S tate Data, as 
well as be responsible for the implementation of any applicable federal, state, or DAS 
Data protection requirements defined by the Data Owner.  
5. The DAS Data Governance Committee (DGC) shall:  
a)  Be composed of the following member or advisory roles, or appropriate designees: 
(i) DAS Director 
(ii) DAS Chief Information Officer 
(iii) Agency Information Security Officer 
(iv) DAS Chief Legal Counsel 
(v) State Chief Privacy Officer 
(vi) DAS Agency Chief Data Officer 
b) Establish, maintain, review, enhance , and e nsure adherence to the DAS Data 
Governance framework. 
c) Meet on a regular schedule to assess and evaluate the impact of Data decisions on 
Data management, DAS process management, compliance management, and 
people management. 
d) If the  DAS DGC determines a matter has broad strategic implications and is best 
resolved with feedback from DAS  executive leadership or the Chief Data Officer 
Council, the DAS DGC will refer the matter to the DAS  executive leadership team 
before making a final determination. 
e) As needed, invite ad hoc Data Owners, Stewards, or Custodians from across DAS to 
represent specific Data requests or issues under consideration.

B. Data Use, Access, or Sharing: All proposed DAS Data use, access, or sharing must be   
reviewed for Confidentiality  implications. Any internal or external initiated requests or 
proposals to use, access, or share DAS Data that contains Sensitive Data, or when combined 
with other d atasets could result in the identification of Sensitive Data, shall provide a 
justification for use, access, or sharing. All Data decisions must comply with federal and state 
statutes and policies. 
 
1. Individuals in DAS Data Governance roles must follow procedures, as defined by the DAS 
Chief Data Officer and DAS Data Governance Committee, that address: 
a) Handling non-routine Data requests involving PII, CPI, or Sensitive Data. 
b) Handling routine Data requests that do not involve PII, CPI, or Sensitive Data. 
2. DAS Data that is approved for publication to the DataOhio Portal or a DAS website shall: 
a) Be vetted for Data Quality and accuracy prior to publication;  
b) Comply with publishing standards and approval protocols established by 
InnovateOhio and/or DAS; 
c) Adhere to state and DAS policies; and, 
d) Ensure Data remains current based on a defined refresh schedule. 
 
C. Data Analytics Projects that I nvolve PII /CPI/Sensitive Data: DAS shall comply with the 
requirements outlined below  unless otherwise authorized by the Governor’s Office or for 
purposes specified in state or federal laws, regulations, guidelines, or grant provisions. 
 
1. The DAS Data Governance Committee and, when necessary, an Institutional Review 
Board, shall review all proposed Data Analytics projects that involve the use of 
PII/CPI/Sensitive Data.  
2. The InnovateOhio Platform (IOP) must be used for any DAS-approved project s in 
accordance with Ohio Administrative Policy IT-19, Data Governance. 
3. DAS-approved Data Analytics projects that involve Generative AI shall be referred to the 
AI Council for final review, as outlined in State of Ohio Policy IT -17, Use of Artificial 
Intelligence in State of Ohio Solutions. 
4. DAS divisions and offices must share Data with IOP in accordance with Executive Order 
2019-15D and ORC 125.32. 
 
D. Data Classification and Labeling 
 
1. DAS shall classify and label Data in accordance with State of Ohio Policy IT -13, Data 
Classification, to identify what Data needs to be protected against unauthorized access  
and misuse, and to determine the extent to which it needs to be secured and controlled.

These classifications shall be regularly reviewed to determine if any adjustments are

2. DAS may leverage Data Catalog  tools to label Data Assets, includ ing business, 
operational, and technical metadata, and assign roles to agency Data Assets. 
 
E. Data Quality: As part of system development lifecycle and program integrity, DAS shall work 
to promote Data Quality, including the improvement of existing Data and Data generated as 
the result of the design phase for new IT or Data collection systems. 
 
F. Data Integrity: DAS shall work to promote Data Integrity , including the Integrity of existing 
Data and newly collected Data. 
 
G. Education and Awareness : DAS shall provide Data  Governance education and awareness 
training to individuals within DAS who  are assigned to the  roles and responsibilities defined 
in Section III.A. of this policy . DAS shall address the following topics as part of its training 
efforts: 
 
1. The DAS Data Governance framework and associated policies , standards,  and 
procedures. 
2. The process for identifying and assigning Data Classification Labels and guidelines for 
State Data. 
3. Data sharing agreement requirements and processes , including Data distribution and 
disclosure guidelines. 
 
H. Compliance: DAS shall align with the requirements of this policy by July 1, 2025, as required 
by State of Ohio Policy IT-19, Data Governance. 
 
IV. Definitions 
 
A. Artificial Intelligence (AI) . Interdisciplinary field, usually regarded as a branch of computer 
science, dealing with models and systems for the performance of functions generally 
associated with human intelligence, such as reasoning and learning.1 
B. Confidentiality. Preserving authorized restrictions on i nformation access and disclosure, 
including a means for protecting personal privacy and proprietary Information.2

C. Confidential Personal Information (CPI) . Personal Information defined by ORC 1347.01 that 
agencies are prohibited from releasing under Ohio's Public Records Law and that is retrieved 
from a system within the scope of ORC 1347.15.  
D. Criticality. A measure of the degree to which an organization depends on the information or 
information system for the success of a mission or of a business function.  For purposes of this 
policy, the elements of Criticality are the organization’s need for Integrity an d Availability of 
the information or information systems. 
E. Data. Coded representation of quantities, objects, and actions  (Note: This includes  Data 
processed into a form that has meaning and value to the recipient to support an action or 
decision). 
F. Data Analytics . Th e management of D ata for all uses (operational and analytical) and the 
analysis of D ata to drive business processes and improve business outcomes through more 
effective decision-making and enhanced customer experiences.3 
G. Data Asset . A piece of information , deemed as valuable,  that is collected and stored by an 
organization (i.e., databases, dashboards, documents, spreadsheets, images, videos , voice, 
etc.). Organizations may use Data Assets to inform their strategy, make business decisions, or 
derive insights.  
H. Data Catalog. A detailed inventory of an organization’s Data Assets that uses standardized 
descriptions and m etadata to help users easily find and understand available D ata Assets. 
Maintaining a catalog helps to ensure that business and information technology users can 
identify and apply the correct Data Assets. 
I. Data Classification Label . Denotes the level of protection required based on the 
Confidentiality and Criticality of Data in accordance with the agency’s risk assessment. Data 
Classification Labels enable policy -based standards for securing and handling Data and 
sharing information among organizations.4 
J. Data Domain. A logical grouping of Data, over which someone has control, influence, or rights 
over. Data Domains are designated, named, and assigned accountability.  
K. Data Governance. The exercise of authority and control over the management of Data Assets.  
This includes planning, monitoring, enforcement, strategy alignment, defining objectives, 
establishing policies, stewardship, cataloging, and labeling agency or enterprise information. 
Data Governance matches stakeholder needs with the appropriate Data Owner(s) within the 
agency to provide the right information.

L. Data Product. A solution that is built for a specific need, leverages valuable datasets, is easily 
accessible and searchable, is secured in accordance with the nature of the Data, and provides 
insights to assist in decision-making. Datasets and visualizations within the DataOhio Portal 
are examples of  Data Products.  
M. DataOhio Portal  or Portal . Resulting from Executive Order 2019 -15D, the DataOhio Portal 
displays public datasets and facilitates the request, approval, and delivery of secured 
datasets, providing public access to i nformation with transparency and ease. The Portal 
improves Ohioan and customer interactions with agencies. 
N. Data Quality. Data that is reliable, trustworthy, and supports the organization. 
O. Generative AI. A kind of AI capable of generating new content such as code, images, music, 
text, simulations, 3D objects, videos, and so on. It is considered an important part of AI 
research and development, as it has the potential to revolutionize many industries, including 
entertainment, art, and design.5 
P. Information Technology Resources. Any information technology resource, such as computer 
hardware and software, IT services, telecommunications equipment and services,  digital 
devices such as digital copiers and facsimile machines, supplies, and the Internet, made 
available to employees, contractors, temporary personnel and other agents of the state in the 
course of conducting state government business in support of agency missions and goals. 
Q. InnovateOhio Platform (IOP) . Establ ished by Executive Order 2019 -15D, the InnovateOhio 
Platform (IOP) provides integrated and scalable capabilities that enable state a gencies to 
become more customer-centric and Data-driven, delivering on InnovateOhio's vision to better 
serve Ohioans. 
R. Institutional Review Board. An administrative body established to protect the rights, privacy, 
and welfare of human subjects recruited to participate in research activities conducted under 
the auspices of the institution with which it is affiliated. The Institutional Review Board is 
charged with reviewing, prior to its initiation, all research (whether funded or not) involving 
human participants. 
S. Integrity. Guarding against improper i nformation modification or destruction, and includes 
ensuring Information non-repudiation and authenticity. 
T. Organizational User. Anyone who is an employee of the state or is otherwise authorized to 
work on behalf of the state and who has a need to access State of Ohio IT systems and has 
secured agency authorization for such access. The organizational identifier will help to 
distinguish the individual during the authentication process. Examples of resources that may 
fall into this category include full-time employees, part-time employees, seasonal employees, 
temporary contract workers, independent contractors or consultants, volunteers, or unpaid

U. Personally Identifiable Information. Information that can be used directly or in combination 
with other information to identify a particular individual. It includes:  
1. a name, identifying number, symbol, or other identifier assigned to a person,  
2. any information that describes anything about a person,  
3. any information that indicates actions done by or to a person,  
4. any information that indicates that a person possesses certain personal characteristics.6 
V. Sensitive Data . Data that is classified  as Confidentiality moderate, Confidentiality high, 
Criticality moderate, or Criticality high in accordance with State of Ohio Policy IT -13, Data 
Classification. There is a high degree of risk when unauthorized release or disclosure is 
contrary to a legally mandated Confidentiality requirement. There may be a moderate risk 
and potentially a high risk in cases of i nformation for which an a gency has discretion under 
the law to release Data, particularly when the release must be made only according to agency 
policy or procedure. The Data may be certain types of Personally Identifiable Information that 
is also sensitive such as medical Information, social security num bers, and financial account 
numbers. It includes Federal Tax Information under IRS Special Publication 1075, Protected 
Health Information under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act, Criminal 
Justice Information under the Federal Bureau of Investigation’s Criminal Justice Information 
Services (CJIS) Security Policy, and the Social Security Administration Limited Access Death 
Master File. The Data may also be other types of information not associated with a particular 
individual such as sec urity and infrastructure records, trade secrets, and business bank 
account Information. 
W. State Data. All data and information provided by, created by, created for, or related to the 
activities of the State and any information from, to, or related to all persons that conduct 
business or personal activities with the State, including, but not limited to Sensitive Data. All 
State Data is and will remain the property of the State and, unle ss specifically provided 
otherwise in the Contract, Contractor acquires no right, title, or interest in or to State Data.
