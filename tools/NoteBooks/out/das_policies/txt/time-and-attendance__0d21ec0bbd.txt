Agency

State of Ohio Administrative Policy

No: HR-24 -
State Human Resources Division

Effective:
September 1, 2023

Issued By:

Kathleen C. Madden, Director

Purpose

To set forth minimum requirements for agency time and attendance policies in accordance with the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) and statewide policy. The first occurrence of a defined term in the policy is in bold, italic type, and is hyperlinked to the definition in Section IV.

Scope

This policy applies to all state agencies, boards, and commissions under the authority of the Governor (collectively referred to as Agency or Agencies).

Policy

The State of Ohio is committed to providing superior customer service and accountability to citizens. There is value and necessity in encouraging statewide standardization in certain areas related to time and attendance policies. Agencies, in consultation with legal counsel, are responsible for complying with the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), state laws, this policy, and applicable collective bargaining agreements regarding time and attendance.

Agencies Must Accurately Maintain Records of Actual Hours Worked

The FLSA requires employers to keep certain records on behalf of their employees. The FLSA does not require a particular form for the records but does require certain identifying information about each employee as well as precise and accurate data about the number of hours worked and wages earned. Additionally, state law requires employers to keep certain records on behalf of their employees. Article II, section 34(a) of the Ohio Constitution and Ohio Revised Code (ORC) 4111.14 require employers in the State of Ohio to maintain certain records, including the hours worked each day and each work week, for at least three (3) years from the date the hours were worked and for three (3) years following the last date the employee was employed.

Recording Hours Worked: The following is a list of timekeeping information that each Agency is required to record:

Time of day the employee begins and ends work on any given day.

Hours employee worked each day.

Total hours employee worked each work week.

A particular method of maintaining these records is not required. Agencies may determine an appropriate method such as electronic time reporting systems. Supervisors who approve time in this manner must act in good faith in verifying the accuracy of the employee’s record.

Agency Responsibility

Appointing authorities are responsible for the implementation and enforcement of time and attendance policies. Agencies must provide training to managers/supervisors and new employees about how to accurately report hours worked and properly maintain records of actual hours worked. Appropriate staff should be able to identify records required for audit purposes and understand how to address common time and attendance problems.

Customer Service Hours

Agencies are responsible for ensuring there is coverage during their established core business hours to perform all their business and administrative functions for both internal and external customers. Business and administrative functions may include, but are not limited to, answering telephones and responding to inquiries from the public, as well as other state entities.

Alternative Work Schedules

Provided an Agency can maintain coverage during the established core business hours, the Agency may offer alternative work schedules. If alternative work schedules are offered, the Agency should incorporate information about the schedules into its time and attendance policy. The policy should include the process for requesting and approving alternative work schedules as well as retain the Agency’s rights to modify or cancel the schedules. For bargaining unit employees, Agencies must consult the applicable collective bargaining agreement for provisions related to alternative work schedules.
