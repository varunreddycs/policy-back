                    IT-19 Data Governance


State of Ohio Administrative Policy

No: IT-19
 - Information Technology

Effective:
 April 29, 2025

Issued By:


Kathleen C. Madden, Director

Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to establish and maintain a formal Data Governance framework that ensures appropriate decision-making and accountability in the creation, consumption, control, and valuation of state Data and Analytics. All defined terms are capitalized and a link to the glossary of definitions is found in section IV.

Scope

This policy applies to all state agencies, boards, and commissions under the authority of the Governor, collectively referred to as Agency or Agencies, and to all Data that is created or maintained by any state entity on Information Technology (IT) Resources.

	This policy is not intended to address Public Records requests, which are defined in Ohio Revised Code (ORC) Section 149.43 (Ohio Public Records Law).

Policy

Agencies shall define and maintain Data Governance frameworks that align with this policy. Agencies shall establish and maintain an Agency Data Governance policy and any applicable related procedures, processes, and metrics, which provide oversight and guide Data Governance activities at all levels. At a minimum, the Agency Data Governance framework shall include and account for the following:

Agency Data Governance Roles and Responsibilities

Organizational Users must responsibly use state data that resides within IT Resources that include:


Personally Identifiable Information (PII),

Confidential Personal Information (CPI),

Confidential Data, and

Data found in, analyzed, or generated by Artificial Intelligence (AI) as described in Ohio Administrative Policy IT-17, “Use of Artificial Intelligence in State of Ohio Solutions.”

The Data Owner is a person responsible for ensuring that all Data and Data products within their domain are protected and managed appropriately pursuant to law, policy, and procedures. The Data Owner is also responsible for authorizing access and/or sharing of Agency Data through the creation of Data use or sharing agreements, in consultation with their designated legal and privacy representatives. The Data Owner is the primary point of contact for regulatory audits and shall work in collaboration with Data Stewards and Data Custodians to provide responses.

The Data Steward is a person responsible for the day-to-day management of a dataset and serves as the subject matter expert who understands and communicates the meaning and use of Information.

The Data Custodian is a person responsible for the safe custody, transport, and storage of state Data, as well as the implementation of any applicable federal, state, or Agency Data protection requirements. The Data Custodian may also be responsible for performing regular Data backups as well as supporting Data Quality and validation efforts, ensuring Confidential Data is encrypted, retaining Data activity logs, and restoring Data.

Other key Agency roles required in Data Governance for overall management and decision-making, and ensuring the appropriate decision rights and accountability in the valuation, creation, consumption, and control of Data and analytics, include:


Chief Data Officer (CDO) or Analytics Lead

Chief Information Officer (CIO)

Chief Legal Counsel

Chief Privacy Officer

Information Security Professional

Data Governance Committee or an established review process, which includes collaboration of the key Agency roles identified in this section who are responsible for making decisions regarding the use and sharing of Data.

If necessary, Agencies may combine or expand upon the Data Governance roles identified in this policy to meet business needs.

Data Use and Sharing

All proposed Agency Data use or sharing must be reviewed for any security, privacy, or Confidentiality implications. Any internal or external initiated requests or proposals to use or share Agency Data that contains protected Information (PII, CPI, Confidential Data), or when combined with other datasets could result in the identification of Confidential Data, shall provide a justification for use or sharing. All Data use or sharing decisions must comply with state and federal statutes and policies.

Each Agency shall establish processes for the review and approval for sharing or using Agency Data, which includes:


Handling non-routine Data requests involving PII, CPI, or Confidential Data.

Handling routine Data requests that do not involve PII, CPI, or Confidential Data.

Agency Data that is approved for publication to the DataOhio Portal or an Agency website, which processes must include:


Vetting for Data Quality and accuracy prior to publication;

Compliance with publishing standards and approval protocols established by InnovateOhio and/or Agency;

Adherence to Ohio Administrative Policy IT-08, “Website Standardization;” and

Continually remain current based on a defined refresh schedule.

Agencies shall develop processes for establishing, managing, and maintaining Data sharing or use agreements.

Data Analytics Projects that Involve PII/CPI/Confidential Data

Agencies shall comply with the requirements outlined below unless otherwise authorized by the Governor’s Office or for purposes specified in state or federal laws, regulations, guidelines, or grant provisions.

The Agency’s Data Governance Committee or designated Agency data governance decision-makers and/or Institutional Review Board shall review all proposed Data analytics projects that involve the use of PII/CPI/Confidential Data.

The State-Managed or Governed Data Ecosystem must be used for any approved project.


Cross-Agency Data analytics projects shall be performed and maintained on the InnovateOhio Platform (IOP) Data Analytics ecosystem, as defined in ORC 125.32 and Executive Order 2019-15D. Agencies shall leverage the Department of Administrative Services (DAS) approved Data Use Agreement template for IOP data analytics projects. (Refer to Section VI. Resources for the template location.)

The projects shall not be performed, shared, or duplicated outside of the State-Managed or Governed Data Ecosystem.

Any Agency approved Generative AI projects shall be referred to the AI Council for final review, as outlined in Ohio Administrative Policy IT-17, “Use of Artificial Intelligence in State of Ohio Solutions.”

Data Classification and Labeling

Agencies shall classify Data in accordance with Ohio Administrative Policy IT-13, “Data Classification,” to identify what Information needs to be protected against unauthorized access, misuse, and the extent to which it needs to be secured and controlled. These classifications shall be regularly reviewed to determine if any adjustments are needed.

Agencies may leverage Data Catalog tools to label Data assets, which include business, operational, and technical metadata, and assign roles to Agency Data assets.

Agencies shall follow the requirements outlined in Ohio Administrative Policy IT-14, “Data Encryption and Securing Confidential Data,” to protect Confidential Data.

Data Quality

As part of system development lifecycle and program integrity, Agencies shall work to promote Data Quality, including the improvement of existing Data and Data generated as the result of the design phase for new IT or Data collection systems.

Education and Awareness

Agencies shall provide Data Governance education and awareness training that is designed to complement the roles and responsibilities defined in Section III.A. of this policy. Agencies shall address the following topics as part of their training efforts:

The Agency’s Data Governance Framework and associated policies and procedures.

The process for identifying and assigning Data Classification Labels and guidelines for state Data.

Data Sharing Agreement requirements and processes, including Data distribution and disclosure guidelines.

Compliance

Agencies shall comply with the Data Governance requirements outlined in this policy within 12 months of the original effective date.

State Roles and Responsibilities

The State of Ohio Chief Data Officer Council, through the Data Governance sub-committee, shall be responsible for:


Establishing and maintaining statewide Data Governance requirements, including those for AI solutions. The requirements shall define information management controls, procedures, and processes for Data set selection, evaluation, and preparation.

Maintaining Data Governance best practices to assist Agencies in establishing their Data Governance framework.

Promoting alignment and standardization of Data Governance practices amongst Agencies.

Agencies that do not have training resources available shall contact the Department of Administrative Services IOP Data Analytics for assistance (refer to Section VII. Inquiries for contact information).
