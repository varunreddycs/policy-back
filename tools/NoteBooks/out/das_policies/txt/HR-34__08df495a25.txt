                  Employee Development Fund for the State of Ohio, Unit 2 Association (HR-34)

 |

Agency

State of Ohio Administrative Policy

No: HR-34 -
State Human Resources Division

Effective:
February 1, 2026

Issued By:

Kathleen C. Madden, Director

Purpose

To administer the Employee Development Fund for qualifying State of Ohio, Unit 2 Association (Unit 2) employees in Bargaining Unit 2 pursuant to Section 36.05 of the Collective Bargaining Agreement. The first occurrence of a defined term is in bold, italic type, and is hyperlinked to the definition in Section IV.

Scope

This policy applies to all state agencies, boards, and commissions under the authority of the Governor (collectively Agency or Agencies) and eligible Agency employees.

Policy

This policy provides for reimbursement to eligible employees from the Unit 2 Employee Development Fund (Fund) for job-related
Professional Development
.

Employee Eligibility

To be eligible for reimbursement pursuant to this policy, the employee must be:

a
Permanent Employee
 covered by the collective bargaining agreement between the State of Ohio and Unit 2;

paid directly by warrant of the director of the Ohio Office of Budget and Management;

in
Active Pay Status
; and,

actively employed by the State of Ohio when a request for reimbursement is submitted.

Approved Medical or Military Leave. Unit 2 EDF eligibility may continue while an employee is on an approved medical or military leave if all other requirements for reimbursement are met, the funds are available and the employee was in Active Pay Status as of the day the Professional Development activity began.

Position Abolishment/Layoff. Eligibility may continue for an employee who is separated from employment or placed in a bargaining unit position due to a position abolishment/layoff if all other requirements for reimbursement are met, including the availability of funds, and the Professional Development activity begins prior to the effective date of the position abolishment/layoff.

Eligible Professional Development Expenses

Professional development is education or training that builds or improves an employee’s capabilities. Professional Development activities include college courses, conferences, continuing education instruction, seminars, and trainings. To be eligible for reimbursement, the Professional Development activity must be job-related unless otherwise approved by management, and must provide knowledge and/or skills that relate to a job classification currently used by the State of Ohio. These major job classifications are listed on the Department of Administrative Services (DAS), State Human Resources Division, Office of Talent Management website. (See Section VI Resources below for the link.)

If grades are given, the employee must attain a “C” or better, or receive a “pass” if assessed on a pass/fail basis.

Non-instructional fees that are required for enrollment in a Professional Development activity, such as application fees, registration fees, required class books, graduation fees, and other related expenses are eligible for reimbursement if they are included on the enrollment invoice from the organization that provided the Professional Development.

For an expanded list of eligible expenses, please see
Attachment 1 : List of Ineligible and Eligible Expenses
.

Ineligible Expenses

Expenses that are not eligible for reimbursement include but are not limited to the following:

travel expenses (e.g., parking, travel, food or lodging expenses);

any non-instructional fees that are not required to enroll in a course (e.g., application fees, drop or late fees, non-instructional field trips, flight instruction, airtime fees, fuel expenses, and other similar expenses);

elective and pre-requisite courses are not eligible for reimbursement unless the employee is enrolled in a degree program and the electives/pre-requisites are part of that degree program;

expenses involving sports, games, or hobbies are not eligible for reimbursement unless the employee is enrolled in a degree program and those courses are part of that degree program;

hardware or commercial software fees, unless hardware or commercial software is required for course participation and completion;

equipment;

subscriptions for periodicals;

software subscriptions;

taxes or fees for shipping and handling;

previously reimbursed expenses – employees may only receive reimbursement for an expense once per fiscal year; or

any other expense from which an Agency is unlikely to benefit.

For an expanded list of ineligible expenses, please see
Attachment 1 : List of Ineligible and Eligible Expenses
.

Reimbursement Eligibility and Limits

Subject to the limitations of the Fund, each eligible employee may receive a maximum of $5,000 per fiscal year for eligible expenses.

Eligible employees that change status during a fiscal year (i.e., take a position covered by a different union or an exempt position) may access the applicable employee development funds immediately. The yearly individual funding limit is based on the employee’s status (e.g., exempt or bargaining unit) at the time of request for reimbursement submission and must be reduced by any amount previously reimbursed to the employee during the present fiscal year from any employee development fund.

Reimbursement is not available for expenses that are fully paid for by scholarship, grant, or Agency funds. For expenses that are partially paid for by scholarship, grant, or Agency funds, reimbursement is not available for the portion of the cost that is paid for by the scholarship, grant, or Agency funds.

Disbursement of funds is contingent upon the availability of funds at the time of request for reimbursement submission.

Request for Reimbursement

Once an activity is satisfactorily completed, an employee may request reimbursement for eligible expenses. Eligible expenses will be reimbursed up to the annual maximum as specified in Section III(D)(1).

Unless otherwise noted in this policy, employees must apply for reimbursement within 90 calendar days of the completion of the course or activity. In the event an employee cannot submit the request within 90 calendar days, the employee should contact DAS at
EDFunds@das.ohio.gov
. The DAS Office of Talent Management Administrator or designee will review extension requests on a case-by-case basis.

DAS will communicate the annual fiscal year deadline for reimbursement request submissions on MyOhio.gov and the EDF website. (See Section VI Resources below for the link.) Submission on or before the annual deadline will ensure that the request is charged against the employee’s reimbursement allotment for the current fiscal year.

To be reimbursed for eligible expenses, an employee must submit a completed reimbursement request along with documentation (or “proof”) to substantiate the cost of the expense(s), to establish the employee paid the expense(s) using personal funds, and to demonstrate the employee completed the Professional Development activity. Typically, electronic forms of proof are acceptable. The following must be submitted:

A completed online Employee Development Fund Request. (See Section VI Resources below for the link.)

Proof of Cost: Employees must submit copies of original invoices and/or receipts of expenses. Documents should be itemized and provide a breakdown of the specific costs for each expense.

Proof of Payment: Employees must submit copies of original receipts or other documentation that establishes the employee paid the cost of the eligible expense(s).

Proof of Completion: To be reimbursed for eligible expenses, an employee must also submit proof of completion. For Professional Development activities, proof of completion must be a certificate of completion, continuing education unit (CEU) certificate, or a letter/email from the provider of the Professional Development activity on letterhead. If the Professional Development activity is a graded course, including pass/fail, the proof of completion must be documentation of the grade. For a graded course provided by a college/university, the documentation must be issued by the registrar of the institution. For graded courses provided by non-college/university organizations, the documentation must be on the organization’s letterhead.

Please refer to the EDF Checklist, included as a link in Section VI below, for explanations and examples of the required documentation. DAS retains the right to request additional documentation as necessary in order to validate the reimbursement request.

All complete and accurate requests will be approved on a first-come, first-served basis provided that funds are available. The application submission date is the date that the submission request is complete and accurate. A request that does not contain sufficient proof as described above is not complete and accurate. Such requests will not be processed until the employee submits the requisite proof.

If an employee submits a request that is incomplete or has errors, the employee will be notified via email that the request was paused. The employee will refer back to the request in the electronic system, which will explain what information is incorrect or missing. The request will remain in a paused status until the missing information is provided or any errors are corrected.

Reimbursement and Tax Withholding

Reimbursement will be included in the employee’s biweekly paycheck. Tax withholding will be done in accordance with the Internal Revenue Code. Additional information can be found in Internal Revenue Service (IRS) Publication 970. (See Section VI - Resources below for the link.)

Amounts over $5,250 reimbursed to an employee in a calendar year for Professional Development activities will be treated as taxable income and corresponding payroll taxes will be withheld from such amounts. If the employee has questions about whether or not such expenses should be treated as taxable income, the employee should consult with their tax professional.

Appeal of Decision Application

If an employee’s Request for Reimbursement is denied, the employee may appeal to the DAS Office of Talent Management Administrator or designee. A link to the appeal form is included in Section VI and is also available on the EDF website on myOhio.gov. If the appeal is denied by the DAS Office of Talent Management Administrator or designee, the employee may appeal to the Deputy Director of the State Human Resources Division. The decision made by the Deputy Director of the State Human Resources Division shall be final.

Any grievance involving denial of reimbursement from the Fund should be sent to the DAS/Office of Collective Bargaining for review.

Definitions

Active Pay Status.
 The conditions under which an employee is eligible to receive pay. This includes, but is not limited to, vacation leave, sick leave, bereavement leave, military leave, compensatory time, holidays, and personal leave. Active Pay Status does not include disability leave, workers’ compensation leave, administrative leave, or other leave of absence except approved education leave or if working to supplement a leave at the time that the request for reimbursement is submitted.

Permanent Employee.
 Any full-time or part-time employee holding a position that requires working a schedule of twenty-six (26) consecutive biweekly pay periods, or any other regular schedule of comparable consecutive pay periods. It does not include intermittent, temporary, seasonal, external interims, student help or individuals covered by personal service contracts.

Professional Development.
 Education or training that develops or improves a bargaining unit employee’s knowledge and/or skills that relate to a job classification currently used by the State of Ohio.

Authority

Collective Bargaining Agreement between the State of Ohio and Unit 2, Section 36.05

Employee Development Fund Checklist 
  
EDF Checklist  1 | Page  
 
Four Required Items 
Four items are required for an Employee Development Fund (EDF) reimbursement request to 
be fully complete and ready for review: 
1. EDF Request for Reimbursement 
2. Proof of Cost 
3. Proof of Payment 
4. Proof of Completion 
 
1. EDF Request for Reimbursement – All fields need to be completed accurately and fully.  
When saving documents to be attached to the request, be sure to include the requesting 
employee’s name and/or SOUID in the file name. 
 
Tools: 
• EDF Job Aid 
• Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) 
2. Proof of Cost – document that displays the cost, fee, or rate charged. 
• Examples:  
o Brochure 
o Web page 
o Letter from the provider on the provider’s letterhead  
o Email that lists the fee(s) charged 
o Course catalog page: 
 Entire catalogs or books are not required 
 Instead, provide the page that contains the course name/event and fee 
• When saving documents to be attached to the request, be sure the requesting 
employee’s name and/or SOUID is included in the file name. 
 
3. Proof of Payment – submit copies of original receipts or other documentation that 
establishes the employee personally paid the cost of the eligible expense(s): 
• Proof establishes what you paid the provider, which may include student loans, 
grants, and/or scholarships.  
• Examples include an invoice or copy of an online account page from the provider 
that shows current charges and full payment with a zero balance for the named 
course/event.  
• If needed, remove or redact all account numbers and other private information. 
• Ensure your name appears on the document.
